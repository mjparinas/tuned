"Is IPTV legal?" is one of the most searched questions about it, and most of the answers online are written by people selling subscriptions. Here's a plain explanation from the people who make a player. It isn't legal advice, and the rules differ from country to country, so check your own.

## IPTV is a technology, not a service

IPTV just means **TV sent over the internet** using the same technology as the rest of the web. A lot of it is completely ordinary:

- Many phone, cable and internet companies deliver their TV packages this way.
- Broadcasters stream their own channels on their websites and apps.
- Free, ad-supported streaming channels publish playlists anyone can use.
- People stream their own recordings and films from a home media server like Plex, Jellyfin or Emby.

So the technology is fine. What matters is **who is sending the channels, and whether they have the right to.**

## A player and a provider are different things

- **A provider** supplies the channels: the playlist, the login, and the streams themselves. Whether a provider is legitimate depends on whether it has licensed what it sends.
- **A player** is an app that plays a playlist you give it, like a web browser opens whatever address you type, or a music app plays your own files.

## Warning signs before you pay for a playlist

A provider is more likely to be unlicensed when:

1. **The price is too good to be true.** Thousands of premium channels, every sports package and every new film for a few dollars a month is the classic sign.
2. **There's no real company behind it.** No business name, address or terms; just a messaging app or a social media page.
3. **It only takes awkward payments**, like gift cards or cryptocurrency, with no refunds.
4. **It sells "lifetime" access** or reseller credits.
5. **The channels come and go**, and support tells you to use a VPN "to avoid blocks".

Watching an unlicensed service can break the law where you live, and those services can vanish overnight with your money. Your safest choice is a provider that clearly holds the rights to what it shows, such as your TV or internet company's own service, a broadcaster's, or free channels published by their owners.

## The short answer

IPTV as a technology is legal and everywhere. A player like Tuned is a tool, the same as a browser or a media player. Whether what you watch is legal depends on your provider, so choose one that has the rights to what it sells.
