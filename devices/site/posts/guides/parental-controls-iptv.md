A provider's playlist is made for everyone, so it often includes adult categories alongside the cartoons. Most players show the whole list to whoever picks up the remote. Tuned is built the other way round: adult categories start hidden, and a kids profile only ever shows kids and family channels.

Tuned is a player for your own playlist and includes no channels or content. These controls decide what Tuned shows from your provider's list.

## Adult categories are hidden from the start

When Tuned saves your playlist, it marks any group or channel whose name says it's adult (XXX, Adult, 18+ and the same words in other languages) and hides it. Nothing to set up.

To show them, go to **Settings › Profiles and parental controls › Show adult categories**. Turning it on asks you to set a **parental PIN** first.

<figure><img src="/blog/img/guides/settings-page-profiles.webp" alt="Tuned's Profiles and parental controls settings with Profiles and Show adult categories, hidden, which asks for a PIN" width="270" height="586" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Settings › Profiles and parental controls.</figcaption></figure>

Even then, the unlock doesn't last:

- It ends **5 minutes after Tuned leaves the screen**, so a phone handed over later shows nothing adult.
- Switching profile locks it again.
- **Five wrong PINs** in a row make you wait 30 seconds.
- The same rule applies everywhere a channel can start: widgets, the Android TV home screen, the quick-settings tile and favourites. A hidden channel can't sneak in through a shortcut.

## Make a kids profile

Profiles give each person their own favourites, Continue Watching and watched marks on the same playlist. Turn on **Kids profile** when you add or edit one, and that profile:

- shows **only kids and family** groups and channels,
- **never** shows adult categories, even with the PIN,
- has **no Settings**, so nothing can be changed from it.

<figure><img src="/blog/img/guides/browse-profiles-named.webp" alt="Who's watching in Tuned with two adult profiles and a profile marked Kids" width="270" height="586" loading="lazy"><figcaption>A kids profile is marked on Who's watching.</figcaption></figure>

Leaving a kids profile asks for the parental PIN, so a child can't just switch to a grown-up's profile. That's also why the first kids profile asks you to set the PIN.

## Lock a profile with its own PIN

Any profile can have its own PIN of 4 to 8 digits. Switching to it, or editing it from another profile, asks for it. Forgot it? **Forgot PIN** takes your playlist's password (or link) instead, which only the account holder knows.

## On the TV, too

With more than one profile, Tuned opens on **Who's watching?**, on the phone and the TV. On a TV, the remote lands on the first profile, so OK alone starts it.

## Hide the rest, too

Parental controls aren't only about adult content. Thousands of channels from countries you'll never watch make it harder for anyone to find their way. In **Settings › Channels and lists**, hide whole countries, languages and groups for everyone.
