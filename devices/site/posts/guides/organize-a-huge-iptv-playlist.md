A provider's playlist is built for everyone, everywhere. Open it for the first time and you might find 50,000 entries: news in thirty languages, the same sports channel five times in different qualities, 24/7 cartoon loops, and groups named in codes like "EXYU" or "LAT". The channels you actually watch are in there somewhere.

This guide turns that into a list you can use, in the order that makes the biggest difference first. It takes about fifteen minutes once, and it stays that way through every refresh.

Tuned is a player for your own playlist and includes no channels. Everything here changes what Tuned shows, never what your provider sends.

## The plan

1. Hide whole countries and languages you'll never watch.
2. Hide the groups and channel types you don't want.
3. Pin the groups you use most.
4. Use filters for everything else, and let them stay chosen.
5. Build favourites, then your own lists.
6. Tidy names, numbers and dead channels.

## 1. Hide countries and languages (the 80% step)

Most of a big playlist is other countries. **Settings › Channels and lists › Countries and languages** lists every country Tuned found in your playlist, each with a switch, and the languages above them. A language covers every country that speaks it: English is the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland and New Zealand together.

Turn off everything you don't watch. It applies to Live TV, films, shows, search and the guide at once, so a 50,000-entry playlist often drops to a few thousand. There's a search box at the top, so "Can" finds Canada without scrolling.

How does Tuned know a channel's country? From the tag providers put in front of it ("UK |", "CA:", "[US]") and the group it's in. Channels with no country at all are never hidden by this, so nothing disappears by accident.

## 2. Hide groups and the kinds of channel you don't want

Next, the groups that are in a language you watch but still not for you: a whole "Radio" group, a "Religious" one, local channels from a region you've never lived in.

- On **Live TV**, choose a group; two chips appear beside it: **Pin to the front** and **Hide this group**. Hide it and it's gone, everywhere.
- In **Settings › Channels and lists**, **Hide 24/7 channels** removes the endless reruns of a single show ("24/7 Friends"), from Live TV, search, films and shows.
- **Adult categories** are hidden from the start, behind a PIN. See [parental controls](/blog/guides/parental-controls-iptv/).

## 3. Pin the groups you use

A pinned group jumps to the front of the chips on Live TV, right after Favourites and Recent, in the order you pinned them. Pin your three or four everyday groups and you'll rarely scroll the chips again. On a TV, the pin and hide chips are full-size, so the remote reaches them in one press.

## 4. Filters that stay chosen

For everything else, there's **Filters**, the second chip on Live TV. It narrows the list by:

- **Type**: sports, PPV and events, news, kids, movie channels, music, documentaries.
- **Quality**: 4K, FHD, HD.
- **What's on now**: only channels showing sport, films, news, kids or documentaries right now, read from the guide.
- **Show only**: channels with a guide, channels with catch-up, or ones you've watched recently.
- **Language** and **country**.

Filters combine, and they **stay chosen** until you change them, even after the app closes, for each profile. So "Sports, FHD, has a guide" becomes your Live TV every time you open it, if that's what you want.

## 5. Favourites, then your own lists

Tap the heart on every channel you watch regularly. Favourites get their own chip at the top of Live TV, a row on the Android TV home screen, and a widget on a phone. **Settings › Channels and lists › Order favourites** puts them in the order you want.

When favourites grow past twenty or so, split them up with your own **lists**, like playlists in a music app: "Sport", "Kids", "Weekend". Each list is its own chip beside Favourites. **Add to list** is on a channel's page; **Manage lists** in Settings renames and deletes them. Lists are part of [Premium](/blog/guides/premium/); favourites are free.

## 6. Tidy names, numbers and dead channels

- **Rename channels and change logos.** "US | SPORTS ONE FHD ᴴᴰ (B)" can become "Sports One". The new name shows everywhere, and search still finds the old one. A refresh never undoes it. (Premium, on phones, tablets and the website.)
- **Channel numbers.** Turn on **Channel numbers** and every live channel gets a number you can type on the remote, plus the last-channel button to flip back, like a cable box.
- **Duplicates are fine.** You don't need to hide the HD copy of a channel you watch in FHD. Tuned treats them as copies: if one stops working it plays the other, and the quality button in the player switches between them.
- **Clear out dead channels.** **Settings › When a channel fails › Channel health › Scan channels** checks every channel, one at a time, and hides the ones that don't work. It can tell a dead channel from a slow internet or a busy server, so a bad moment doesn't hide good channels.

## Finding anything else: search

Search reads channel names and the TV guide, so typing a team, a show or a film finds the channels showing it now and later, not only channels with that word in their name.

## It all survives refreshes and new devices

Hidden countries and groups, pins, favourites, lists, renames and fixes are kept through every refresh. With a [Tuned account](/blog/guides/premium/) they're in your backup too, so a new TV starts as tidy as your phone.
