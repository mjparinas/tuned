Most IPTV players were built for a TV box first and squeezed onto a phone later. On a phone you're watching on the bus, at lunch, or from bed, so what counts is different: it has to open fast, work with one thumb, keep going when you lock the screen, and hand the picture to the TV when you get home.

First, the part every honest guide should say: **an IPTV player includes no channels.** Tuned, like every app on this page, plays the playlist you already get from your own provider (an Xtream Codes login, an M3U link or file, or a MAC portal). If an app comes with channels built in, it isn't just a player.

## What to look for on a phone

1. **It opens on your channels, not a loading screen.** A phone app gets opened and closed all day. Waiting 20 seconds for a playlist to download every time adds up.
2. **A real TV guide.** "What's on now" under every channel, a full grid, and search that finds a programme, not just a channel name.
3. **One-thumb controls.** Big targets, swipes for brightness and volume, and buttons in plain sight rather than behind a three-dot menu.
4. **It keeps playing.** Picture-in-picture when you switch apps, sound with the screen locked, and a mini player while you browse.
5. **Casting.** Send the channel to a Chromecast or smart TV and let the phone become the remote.
6. **Downloads for offline.** Films and episodes saved for a flight or a commute.
7. **It respects your provider's limit.** Many providers allow one connection at a time. An app that previews channels or downloads in the background can get your login blocked.

### Cast it to the TV

Tap the cast button to send the channel or film to a Chromecast, a Google TV or a smart TV that supports DLNA. The phone stops its own stream first, so your provider still sees one connection, and the phone turns into a remote that says what the TV is doing.
