# Architecture

**Native Kotlin, not Capacitor (2026-11-17).** Melvin asked for "the same tech stack as Liftsmith for Android" and for an "android native app". Liftsmith's Android app is a Next.js web app in a Capacitor shell. For a video player that shell is the wrong tool: HTML5 video in a WebView can't play MPEG-TS (the format IPTV servers send), has no control over buffering, track selection or caption styling, and picture-in-picture is awkward. So the app is native (Kotlin, Compose, Media3) and the _tooling_ follows Liftsmith: docs, branches, CI shape, release flow, Dependabot, formatting and lint.

**Modules.** `:core` is plain Kotlin with no Android dependency: parsers and rules, tested on the JVM in seconds. `:app` is the Android app.

**Data flow.** Sign-in saves the login (encrypted) and downloads the playlist and guide once in the foreground. `SyncRepository` streams each download into Room in short batches, so nothing waits on a refresh; the screens read Room through Paging, so they never wait on the network. WorkManager refreshes both in the background on their own schedules.

**No dependency injection framework.** One `AppContainer` built in `TunedApp` holds the database, OkHttp client, settings, login store and scheduler, created lazily. The app is small enough that Hilt would add build time and start-up cost for nothing.

**Player on Views.** `PlayerActivity` uses Media3's `PlayerView` (Views), not Compose, because it is Media3's own tested surface with the standard controls (captions button, audio and speed menu), and it starts fastest. Everything else is Compose.

**Widgets and tile** (2026-11-17; redesigned 2026-11-19, Melvin: "How do we make this widget look better? Channel logo? Add the tuned logo? What else?", then "build all those widget enhancements"). Two Glance home screen widgets (`widget/`), each laid out for the size it's given (`SizeMode.Exact`):

- **Favourite channels**: the profile's favourites as channel logo tiles, each with the programme on now and a thin bar for how far through it is; a tap starts the channel. A small square shows one channel, a short wide strip shows a row of logos beside the Tuned mark, and anything taller shows the Tuned mark and name over a grid of as many tiles as fit (no scrolling: order them in Settings › Order favourites). Grid tiles keep the size they have on a 4 by 2 widget, so making the widget taller or wider adds rows and columns of channels instead of stretching the tiles (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "I like the size of the channels when the widget is smaller. You can double the number of available channels if you don't resize them"). Names lose the provider's country tag and picture quality ("CA| CP24 HD" is "CP24", `ChannelNames` in `:core`, tested); only tags `Regions` knows come off, so "CBC - Toronto" stays whole. A channel without a logo shows its initials.
- **Continue watching** (optional, added from the widget list): films and episodes in progress, newest first, with poster, TMDB's episode name when the device already knows it, a progress bar and the minutes left; a tap picks up where it was left. One title when small, posters side by side when large.
- **Both**: the Tuned mark opens the app (without the start-on-last-channel jump). They follow Settings › Appearance (theme, accent, pure black), and the wallpaper's colours when Dynamic colour is on (Material You, Android 12+), so there is no separate widget setting. With nothing to show they say so with a button into the app ("Add favourites", or "Open Tuned" when signed out). Logos and posters come through the app's image loader, from the device's cache when the lists already showed them, as small software bitmaps to stay inside the launcher's budget. They redraw after each background refresh, when Tuned or the player goes to the background, on sign-out, and every 30 minutes. They hold no data of their own, so sign-out has nothing to clear but redraws them.

A quick-settings tile, Last channel, goes straight back to the live channel watched last, or opens the app if there isn't one.
