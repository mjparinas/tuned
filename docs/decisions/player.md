# Player

**Media3 ExoPlayer** in `PlayerActivity`, full screen, system bars hidden. A tap shows the standard controls, a back button (Melvin, 2026-11-17) and the title (channel name with what's on now and next, or the movie or episode name); they hide again after the timeout in Settings (3 seconds by default). The title drops the provider's language tag ("EN - Top Gun: Maverick" reads "Top Gun: Maverick", through `Titles.display`), and the line beneath it never shows the category ("|EN| ACTION/THRILLER"): it holds what's on now and next, an episode's show and number, or a catch-up recording's channel, and is gone otherwise (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "remove the language prefix from the title. Remove the category").

**Controls.** Play and pause, seek bar and skip back and forward (on-demand only; lengths in Settings), previous and next (the next channel in the same category for live, the next episode for shows), the subtitle search and subtitle timing buttons just before the CC button, so everything about captions sits together (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "move the sub title search along with the subtitle delay buttons down, next to the CC button"), and the settings menu with audio track and speed. **Playback speed** (0.25× to 2×, Media3's own list) is for films, episodes, catch-up and recordings only: live TV, including a paused live channel, never offers it, and neither does a cast to a TV (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "do 7 (remove that for live tv)"). `ViewPlayer` takes the speed command away there, so the menu row and the media session both leave it out.

**At a glance** (Melvin, 2026-11-19, from a photo of another player: "I like part of this players UI, scrubber with blue highlights (accent colour), I like the current time (don't need the day of the week or date), I also like the pills with the resolution, fps and sound codec"). Three things from that player, and nothing else of its look, on phone and TV (`player/PlayerGlance.kt`): the seek bar's played part and thumb are in the app's accent (Settings › Appearance, or the wallpaper's colour with dynamic colour on; the dark theme's shade, since the player is always dark), with what has loaded a paler shade of it; the time of day sits at the top right after the tool buttons, time only ("10:14 PM", or "22:14" when the device uses 24-hour time), never the weekday or date; and small pills under the title give the picture size, frame rate, sound codec and speakers ("1080p", "24 FPS", "AAC", "STEREO"; 5.1 and 7.1 by name). The picture size uses its everyday name (4K, 1440p, 1080p, 720p, 576p, 480p, 360p; a wide film cropped to 1920x800 is still 1080p), and the size itself only when it has none (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "instead of 1920x1080, can you change that to just 1080p. samething for 720p, 4k, basically use the user friendly names. use resolution if there isn't one"), read from what ExoPlayer is playing. A pill whose value the stream doesn't give is left out (TS channels often don't say their frame rate), and the pills are gone while casting, since the phone plays nothing then. Frame rates are rounded as players show them (23.976 reads 24). All three show and hide with the controls, so the video is otherwise bare, and TalkBack reads the pills in words ("24 frames per second", "Stereo").

**Player tools** (Melvin, 2026-11-17). Buttons at the top right, shown with the controls. Cast, record, channels, flip back, quality, fit and lock, each its own button, with no menu behind three dots (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "On the player I don't like the 3 dots. Get rid of stream info, get rid of open in another player. Make fit a button"). The **More** menu of 2026-11-18 is gone: fit and lock came out of it as buttons, and stream info and open in another player were removed. Channels, flip back and quality show only when they apply, so most of the time the row is cast, record, fit and lock. Subtitle search and timing stay beside CC as he asked. The same row is used on phones, tablets and TVs, and on a TV the arrows reach every button once the controls show.

- **Channels** (live, when the category has more than one): the category's channels down the right, each with its logo on the left (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "Channel lists should show logos on the left"; through the app's image cache, decoded at the 40 dp it's drawn) and what's on now; picking one switches on the same connection.
- **Fit** (its own button since 2026-11-19): Fit, Zoom to fill (crops) and Stretch, in turn, with a short note naming the new choice. TalkBack reads the button with its current choice ("Fit: Zoom to fill"). The last choice is remembered. Greyed out while casting. **Zoom and Stretch change a picture that is already the screen's shape** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "On the Android TV app, it seems like the stretch or fit to screen functionality doesn't work"). Media3's resize modes only act when the picture's shape differs from the screen's, and most channels are 16:9 like a TV, so on Android TV all three looked the same. When the shapes match (within 1%), the bars are inside the picture (a 4:3 show or a widescreen film sent as 16:9), so Zoom enlarges the video by 4:3, which takes either kind of bar off, and Stretch widens it by 4:3, like a TV's own Wide setting (`core/FitScale`, with tests). Only the video surface is scaled, so captions stay whole; it's worked out again when the video's size or the screen changes (rotation, the guide docking), and never in picture-in-picture. When the shapes differ (a 4:3 channel on a TV, 16:9 on a 20:9 phone) Media3's own modes do it, as before.
- **Quality** (the HQ button, Melvin, 2026-11-18: "does any stream have an hq version? otherwise the hq button is useless"): providers list a live channel once per picture quality ("US| ESPN HD", "US| ESPN FHD", "US| ESPN 4K"), so the button lists the best copy of each quality (4K, FHD, HD, SD, or Standard when the name has no tag) and switches to the one picked in the same spot of the list. The old stream closes before the copy opens, so there is still one connection. The quality is read only from the tags at the end of the name (`ChannelCopies.picture`), including stacked and small-capital tags ("HD ᴿᴬᵂ", "ᶠᴴᴰ") and past "[LIVEEVENT]"-style tags; "8K EXCLUSIVE" in the middle of a PPV name doesn't count, and "HQ" is part of a name ("SKY SPORTS NEWS HQ"), never a tag. Copies are the same ones backup copies use (same base name and country, hidden channels left out), but the button works with backup copies switched off. A stream with several renditions of its own (HLS) lists those plus Auto instead. With neither, the button is hidden, which is most channels on a playlist without quality copies. Not while casting.
- **Stream info**: removed 2026-11-19 (Melvin: "Get rid of stream info"). The pills under the title already give the picture size, frame rate, sound codec and speakers. The remote's info key now shows the controls, with those pills.
- **Open in another player**: removed 2026-11-19 (Melvin: "get rid of open in another player"). Don't add it back.
- **Lock** (its own button since 2026-11-19; phones and tablets only, since a TV remote has no touches to ignore): ignores every touch and back, so a pocket or a child can't change anything. A tap shows the unlock button for three seconds.

**Captions** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "customizing CC controls in the settings"). Settings › Captions sets on or off, language, size, font, bold, text colour, edge style and colour, background colour and opacity, distance from the bottom, and whether to ignore the stream's own styling. A live preview shows the result. The CC button changes captions for the current video only.

**Subtitle timing** (Melvin, 2026-11-17). A button beside CC moves the captions earlier or later in steps of 0.1 s and 1 s, up to 30 s either way, for files whose captions don't match the voices. Media3 reads captions as the stream loads, so a `SubtitleParser.Factory` wrapper adds the offset to every cue, and changing it reloads the stream at the same spot (the old connection closes first, so there is still only one). The offset lasts until the player closes, because a bad match belongs to one file. It works on captions Media3 parses (SRT, WebVTT, SSA, TTML, including those inside MKV, MP4 and HLS); CEA-608 captions carried inside a live TS stream aren't moved. The button is greyed out and disabled while subtitles are off, and usable again as soon as a subtitle track is picked, with the CC button or subtitle search (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "Disable subtitle delay if subtitles are disabled"), on phones, tablets and TVs alike. It is also greyed out while casting, where the TV draws the captions.

**Subtitle search** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "add the subtitle search for streams that have no subtitles"). Films and episodes can fetch subtitles from OpenSubtitles.com with the app's own API key, built in from the `OPENSUBTITLES_API_KEY` repository secret by the release workflow, the same way as the TMDB key (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "can we do the same thing with the tmdb keys"). It is never committed or typed into Settings. A build without it (debug builds, forks) hides the player's search button and Settings › Playback › Subtitles, and asks nothing. The button lists matches in the caption language from Settings (English when it says any), best first: subtitles a person wrote before machine translations, then the most downloaded. A film is searched by its TMDB id when the device already has one from its details page, otherwise by its cleaned title and year; an episode by its show, season and number. The picked file is added as a side track and the video reloads at the same spot (one connection, as with subtitle timing, which also works on it). With captions on, a video whose stream has no subtitles fetches the best match by itself (Settings › Playback › Subtitles › Find subtitles automatically, on by default), once per video. Every file is kept in app storage by item, so reopening a film shows its subtitles with no download; OpenSubtitles allows only a few downloads a day without an account, and an optional OpenSubtitles account (kept encrypted with the login, see [security](security.md)) raises that. It works while casting too (2026-11-19): the picked subtitles go to a Chromecast with the video (see Cast below); the search doesn't run by itself then.

**Skip intro** (Melvin, 2026-11-17). IPTV servers don't say where an episode's intro is, so a "Skip intro" button shows at the bottom right during the first part of each episode and jumps ahead by Settings › Player › Skip intro button (85 s by default; Off hides it). Tuned also learns each show: a skip forward by hand near the start (from the first 4 minutes, by 10 s or more, landing within the first 6) is saved as that show's intro, and taps of skip-forward a few seconds apart add up to one. From then on the button shows from 15 s before that intro starts until it ends, and jumps to its end. A skip by hand hides the button for the rest of that episode, and what it teaches is used from the show's next episode, so the button never follows someone skipping ahead. An intro longer than 2 minutes isn't believed: skips in a row that run past that are fast-forwarding, so that episode teaches nothing, and a longer one saved before this rule is ignored in favour of the Settings length (Melvin, 2026-11-19: the button was still up at 2:00 with Skip intro set to 30 s). The player's own seek back after casting doesn't count as a skip. The learned intros live in their own small DataStore (`IntroStore`), outside settings and sync. Only episodes get the button, and not while casting.

**Audio language.** Settings › Playback › Preferred audio language; the player picks a matching track when the stream has one, and the settings menu switches tracks while watching.

**Start speed.** TS streams use the extractor flags that allow non-IDR key frames and detect access units, so live channels start sooner. Buffering has three modes (Fast start, Balanced, Smooth). OkHttp is the data source, shared with the rest of the app, with a user agent picked from common players or typed in ([settings](settings.md)).

**Live streams that drop** are retried quietly up to five times (1.5 s, 3 s and so on to 7.5 s) before an error shows; a live channel that never showed a picture gets one retry before its backup copies are tried. Falling behind the live window jumps back to live.

**The channel you open is the one that plays** (2026-11-18). Next and previous walk the channel's category as the lists show it, so a channel the category hides (one the scan found dead, a 24/7 channel with those hidden, a hidden country) is put first in the queue when it is opened from Favourites, Search, the guide or the widget. Before, the player fell back to the category's first channel and played something else.

**Resume.** Movies and episodes save their position every 10 seconds and on leaving; reopening resumes after 30 seconds in, unless 95% watched. Shows mark watched episodes.

**Cast** (Chromecast, 2026-11-17). The cast button sits with the player tools. Casting uses the default media receiver. When a cast starts the phone stops its own stream, so the provider only ever sees one connection, and the TV plays the channel. Next, previous, swipes and the mini guide change the channel on the TV, and the controls drive the TV (see "Controls while casting" below). Ending the cast resumes on the phone, at the same point for films and episodes. The default receiver can't play raw MPEG-TS, so Xtream live links are sent as the same channel's `.m3u8`; films in formats the receiver lacks (MKV, for example) may not play. DLNA and UPnP TVs (2026-11-17) appear in the same picker, found with an SSDP search on the local network while the player is open; phones without Google Play services list only those. A DLNA TV is sent the link as it is (most play MPEG-TS and MP4), with the same one-connection rule. A TV that can't seek starts films from the beginning. The phone closes its own stream before the TV is sent the link (2026-11-18, after a cast to an Nvidia Shield connected but showed "No media selected"): with both open for a moment, an account allowed one connection turns the TV away. A load the TV turns down is tried once more after 3 s, since a provider can count a closed connection for a moment; if that fails too, the note over the video says the TV couldn't play it. **Saved films and episodes cast from the phone** (Melvin, 2026-11-18: casting "also happens with downloaded episodes"). A TV can't open a file on the phone, and giving it the provider's link would stream what is already saved, so the phone serves the file to the TV over Wi-Fi (`core/FileServer`: HTTP with byte ranges for seeking, open to other web pages for the Chromecast, only the files shared and under a random path). It works without the provider, but needs the phone on the same Wi-Fi as the TV and the player left open (leaving it ends the cast, since the phone is the source). Without a Wi-Fi address the note over the video says so. **A Chromecast gets the saved file as a stream the phone makes while it plays** (2026-11-18, Melvin's logs: a saved MKV episode loaded on an Nvidia Shield and stopped at once with decode error 102; then, after a first version copied the whole file into an MP4 before casting, "can we do on the fly encoding instead of doing the full conversion first?"). A Chromecast's player is a web page: it plays H.264 and HEVC with AAC sound, but not MKV or the Dolby and DTS sound most TV rips carry. So the phone serves the file as HLS made on the fly (`core/CastHls`, `core/Fmp4`, `core/NalUnits`, `player/CastStream`, `player/SoundPieces`, with tests): a playlist of pieces about six seconds long, cut at key frames found with one seek per piece, so it is ready in well under a second, and each piece (fragmented MP4) is made only when the TV asks for it, the pictures copied as they are and the sound copied when it is stereo or mono AAC, otherwise turned into stereo AAC. Casting starts at once with no "getting ready" wait, a seek makes only the piece it lands in, and nothing is written to the phone's storage (the full copy and its cache are removed). **Surround sound goes to a Chromecast as stereo** (2026-11-18: on Melvin's Shield every film and episode, from the provider and from the phone alike, stopped at once with error 102, while live channels, which are mostly stereo, played): 5.1 and 7.1 are mixed for two speakers (centre and surrounds at -3 dB, the low frequency channel left out, peaks clamped). The sound encoder carries on from one piece to the next, so there are no seams while the TV plays straight through, and starts afresh just before a piece the TV seeks to. Films and episodes streamed from the provider still go to the TV as the provider's link, since the phone can't change a stream it isn't reading. Other sound tracks aren't sent; the TV gets the first sound track. Video an MP4 can't hold (VP9, MPEG-2) goes as the file itself. When the TV reports it can't play something, the note over the video says so, and the log names the receiver's error, every detail of each track (profile, level, size, colour, channels), how long the playlist took and how long each piece took to make, so a refusal can be matched to what the TV was given. DLNA TVs play MKV and Dolby, so they still get the file unchanged. A load of the same item within 2 s is ignored (opening the player while a cast started sent it twice, and the second cancelled the first), and a load that a newer one replaced isn't retried. **A cast shows it is working from the first tap** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "when i click the cast button, the user doesn't show any thing until it starts player, can you add a spinner or some other visual indicator that something is happen"). The TV's app takes a few seconds to open and the TV's player a few more, so the note over the video shows a spinner with "Connecting to SHIELD…" as soon as a TV is picked, then "Starting on SHIELD…" while the phone hands over (and gets a saved file's stream ready) and the TV's player starts, and "Playing on SHIELD" once the TV plays or is paused (for DLNA, once the TV accepts Play). The words carry the state, not only the spinner, and the note is a polite live region, so TalkBack reads each step. If the TV can't play it the note says so; if the TV can't be reached, or the picker is cancelled, the note goes, a short message says so and the phone carries on; ending the cast clears it. Opening the player mid-cast shows "Playing on …" straight away. **A stopped cast doesn't take the next show** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "when you're casting a show and you stop it, and you start another show, don't keep trying to cast"): stopping a video on the TV leaves its cast app connected with nothing playing, so opening another show used to send it there too. Now a show opened while the TV is idle or has no video ends the cast and plays on the phone (`CastResume`, with a test); a TV still playing or paused carries on as before. Ending the cast closes the TV's app, so downloads carry on too. DLNA TVs say nothing back, so they can't be told apart this way. **Closing the player ends the cast** (Melvin, 2026-11-19, asked whether leaving the player while the TV plays should end the cast: "Yes"): backing out of the player stops the TV and closes its app (for DLNA, Stop and letting go of the TV in the picker), so the next show plays on the phone and downloads carry on. Leaving the app with Home, locking the phone or turning it leaves the TV playing, and opening the player again picks it up. The cast button's own animation while connecting is the Cast library's. **The phone while casting is a remote** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "when I look at the app, it just says playing on shield, and then the spinner is actively spinning. I can actually tap it and see the playback controls. Can you think of a better UX for casting?"). The phone plays nothing while a TV does, so it no longer shows the frame it stopped on with a note over it (`player/CastingScreen.kt`). From the moment a TV is picked until the cast ends: the video is covered by black with the channel's logo or the film's poster blurred and dimmed behind; above the centre controls, "Casting to SHIELD" with the logo, and under it what the TV is doing in words ("Connecting to SHIELD…", "Starting on SHIELD…", "Playing", "Paused", "Loading on SHIELD…", "Finished", or what went wrong), read out by TalkBack as it changes; the controls stay up the whole time, with no tap to show them, no timeout and a tap not hiding them; and below the centre controls the TV's volume (a Chromecast; a DLNA TV says nothing back, so it has none) and a Stop casting button, which ends the cast and carries on on the phone from where the TV was, like the cast button's own Stop casting. Every note above that used to show over the video while casting now shows on that line instead. **A spinner shows only while something is actually loading**: while the cast connects and starts, and while the TV reports it is loading or buffering; never once the TV plays. The endless spinner was Media3's own buffering spinner: it follows a player's state-change callbacks, which the controls' stand-in for the TV (`ViewPlayer`) never sent, so it stayed as it was the moment the phone stopped; the TV's changes now send them (with a test), and Media3's spinner is off while casting anyway. The title and the tools stay at the top as before. Android's notification and lock screen controls while casting are the Cast library's (play and pause, Stop casting), as before. There is no cast bar on the app's other screens: closing the player ends the cast (below), so they are never open over a cast. **Controls while casting** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "While casting can you show the time and allow it to scrub through the video. Let the play button pause and play the video"). The phone's own player is stopped while the TV plays, so the controls used to show 00:00 and a play button that did nothing visible. Now the controls read the TV (`ViewPlayer` over `TvControls`, which `CastControl` answers from the Cast library's status): the time and length of a film or episode on the TV, the seek bar and the skip back and forward buttons moving it, previous going to the start first by the TV's position, and play and pause showing and switching what the TV does. A video that failed or was stopped on the TV is sent again from where it was when play is pressed. Live channels show play and pause only, as on the phone. A DLNA TV answers nothing back, so it gets play and pause without a time. **What can't work while casting is greyed out** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "Any functionality that you cant use on the UI just disable them, and make them greyed out"), in its place and disabled rather than hidden or answering with a message (`usable` in `PlayerChrome`): subtitle timing, quality, record and fit; and the settings button, since speed and sound tracks don't apply to the TV. TalkBack reads them as disabled. **Subtitles on a Chromecast** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "Is there a way to support subtitles while streaming?"). The TV's player reads WebVTT from a web address, so the subtitles the phone has for a film or episode (from subtitle search, kept as SubRip) are turned into WebVTT as the TV asks for them (`core/WebVtt`, with tests, with the subtitle timing worked in) and served from the phone like a saved file, as the video's one text track. They show when captions are on, and the CC button turns them on and off on the TV. Subtitles picked with subtitle search while casting send the video again with them, from where the TV was. Subtitles inside a provider's stream or a saved MKV aren't sent: the TV's player can't read them from those files. It needs the phone on the same Wi-Fi as the TV, as for saved files. **Cast logging** (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "Can you add casting logging"): every step goes to Settings › Updates and advanced › Logs with logins masked, the connection to the TV, the address and type sent, whether the TV took it, its playing state and any media error it reports, the ranges the TV reads from a saved file, and for DLNA each command and the TV's HTTP answer.

**Picture-in-picture** when leaving the app while playing (Settings, on by default). Leaving without it ends playback. It is the same single stream in a small window, which Melvin keeps (2026-11-17: leaving the app with the picture still showing "is okay"). A second channel inset over the video is ruled out, because it would be a second stream ([scope](scope.md)). Picking another channel while the small window is open (from the app, the widget, the quick-settings tile or a game alert) switches the player to it (2026-11-18; before, the pick was ignored because the player is single-task and the old channel kept playing): the player is closed and opened again on the new pick, so the old stream ends before the new one opens. Picking what is already playing leaves it alone.

**Media buttons** (2026-11-18, from the gap review Melvin asked for: "what else are we missing"). The player screen has a Media3 media session (`player/PlayerSession.kt`), so play, pause, next and previous on headphones, Bluetooth devices and a TV remote, "Hey Google, pause", and the system's now-playing card all control the player on screen. The session wraps the same player as the on-screen controls, so a button press does exactly what the matching button does: pausing live TV pauses into the copy on the device, play while casting plays on the TV, and next or previous changes channel on the one connection. It lives and dies with the player screen. Settings › Player › Media buttons, on by default, turns it off for anyone whose headphones start things by themselves; with background play on, the session stays for the notification, so the buttons work then too.

**Mini player on Back** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "on the android tv and the app, when i'm streaming something on live tv, and then click back, i want the video to continue playing in a small video somewhere out of the way for the user so that we can still watch something as we are browsing for a new show to watch. dont forget that we only want 1 stream at a time so when i find something i like and click on it, it should stop the mini live tv from streaming first before it streams the new show"). Back on a live channel shrinks the player into Android's picture-in-picture window, in a corner over the app (bottom right on a phone, top right on a TV), and the app's lists show underneath to browse (`player/MiniPlayer`, with tests). It is the same player screen, player and connection, so entering the mini player makes no new provider request, and downloads and channel scans keep waiting as they do while anything plays. It is the one stream shrinking, not a second video, so it keeps to [scope](scope.md). Picking anything else from the app while the window shows (a channel, a film, an episode, catch-up, a recording or an event to tune) closes the window first, waits until its stream has hung up (at most 3 s), and only then opens the pick full screen; downloads don't start in that gap. Picking the same channel again, or tapping the window (on a TV, the system's picture-in-picture menu, a long press of Home), brings it back to full screen. Back on the app's first screen closes the window before Back leaves the app, which is a TV remote's way to stop it; the window never takes the remote's focus, so browsing works as usual. On a phone the window's own close button and dragging it away work too. Back closes the player as before for films, episodes, catch-up and recordings, while casting (closing the player ends the cast), when paused, when the player was opened straight from a widget, tile or alert with no lists under it, on a device without picture-in-picture, or when picture-in-picture is turned off for Tuned in Android's settings. Settings › Player › Mini player on Back, on by default, separate from Picture-in-picture (which is for leaving the app). The device smoke test plays a channel, goes Back, checks the stream carried on with no new request, picks another channel, and checks the provider never saw two connections and that Back on Live TV closes the window.

**Keep playing in the background** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "do 3, but have a setting that can toggle this off"). Leaving the app or locking the screen used to end playback. Now the sound carries on, with Android's media notification (title, play and pause, next and previous channel or episode) and the lock screen controls; a tap on the notification goes back to the player as it was (`player/BackgroundPlay.kt`, `PlaybackService`, a Media3 `MediaSessionService` that shows the player screen's own session). It is the same player on the same connection, so nothing new opens and downloads keep waiting until it ends. The service starts while the player is on screen, so Android allows it to carry on, and the notification shows whenever the player plays. It ends playback as before when: the setting is off; something is cast; the person closed the picture-in-picture window (with the screen still on; locking the screen while the window shows carries on); playback is paused out of sight for 10 minutes, so a forgotten pause doesn't hold the provider's connection; or Tuned is swiped away from recent apps (which stops the stream rather than pausing it, since a paused live channel keeps receiving into its rewind copy). The player holds a network wake lock only while playing with the setting on. Settings › Player › Keep playing in the background, on by default.

## Gestures and reconnecting (2026-11-17)

Melvin asked for auto-reconnect and player gestures. Both are on by default and each has a switch in Settings › Player.

- **Gestures** (`PlayerGestures`), as most phone players do them:
  - drag up or down on the left half for brightness, on the right half for volume;
  - double-tap the left or right third to skip back or forward (films and episodes);
  - swipe sideways to change channel (live TV);
  - a single tap shows the controls, and a tap while they show hides them; each tap toggles them once (Melvin, 2026-11-18: tapping hid them and they popped back up).

  Brightness goes back to the system's when the player closes.

  While brightness or volume changes, a thin bar with its icon shows at that edge, brightness on the left and volume on the right, and fades out shortly after the finger lifts (`LevelBars`; Melvin, 2026-11-17: "subtle bars on the left and right side with a brightness and sound icon" instead of text in the middle). The volume icon turns to muted at zero. The bars read out the percentage to screen readers.

- **Reconnect.** A dropped live stream, or a network error on a film or episode, is retried up to five times with a growing pause, and "Reconnecting… (2 of 5)" shows meanwhile. The count resets once it plays again. When the phone's connection comes back (`ConnectivityManager` default network), a stopped stream starts again by itself, and `prepare()` keeps a film's position. It still uses one connection at a time, never a second stream.

**Ratings and trailers** (2026-11-17). From The Movie Database. The app's own key is built in from the release workflow's secret `TMDB_API_KEY` (a v4 read token or a v3 key), so nobody types one in (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "can we do the same thing with the tmdb keys", after Trakt and Google sign-in moved to build secrets). It is never committed; builds without it (debug and pull request builds) turn TMDB off. The old Settings › Ratings field is removed, and a key saved there is deleted from the device and from synced copies. Titles are cleaned of the provider's tags (`EN -`, `4K`, `[FHD]`) and the year is used when the name has one. Without a key nothing is asked and the page says so.

**Film page** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "when i click on a movie, it should take me to the movie details page", and the rating at the bottom right of each poster). Tapping a film's poster, in the grid or in search, opens its page: the backdrop across the top fading into the page, the poster and title over its lower edge, rating, year and runtime, genres, Play (Resume when started), Trailer (YouTube), the summary and a row of the cast with photos. Play now stays in the poster's long-press menu as a shortcut. The page has every action a poster's long press offers as a button, so a remote never needs that long press (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "Optimize TV for remotes, avoid 3 dot drop downs"): Play, Trailer, Download, Watch later, Watched, the favourite heart and Add to list, wrapping to a second line on a narrow phone. Each poster in Movies and Shows shows its TMDB rating in the bottom right corner (TMDB's own score; IMDb's needs a separate service). A show's page has the same details above its episodes. A tall picture fills its tile; a square or wide one (a channel logo the provider sent as the poster) is shown whole and centred instead of cropped, because cropping blows a small logo up two or three times and makes it blurry (Melvin, 2026-11-18; `core/PosterArt`).

**Kept on the device** (Melvin, 2026-11-17). Every TMDB answer is saved in the database (`metadata` table): the search answer for a poster's rating, and the full details once a page is opened. A page or badge seen once loads from the device, works offline, and is asked again only after a month (a week for "not found"). Artwork (posters, backdrops, cast photos, logos) goes through Coil's 512 MB disk cache in app storage rather than the cache folder, so Android doesn't empty it when space is low.

**Episode screenshots** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "For the show like the Simpsons, are there screenshots of the episode?"). Each episode row on a show's page shows TMDB's screenshot at 16:9 beside its TMDB name ("Kamp Krusty", not the provider's "EN - The Simpsons (1989) S04 E01"), runtime and a two-line summary, with the watched check and download button as before and the progress bar along the screenshot's bottom. Only the season on screen is asked for, one request per season, saved in `tmdb_seasons` and refreshed after a week (a season still airing gains episodes). A plain tile stands in while a screenshot loads and when TMDB has none, so rows never jump. Episodes match by season and episode number; without a match the row keeps the provider's name without its language tag. The season's "Download season" and "Mark season watched" sit in a flow row, so the second moves to its own line rather than squeezing.

**Episode names everywhere** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "if you can find the actual name of an episode, prioritize that over showing the season number and episode number"). Continue Watching cards read "The Simpsons · Kamp Krusty" instead of "The Simpsons · S4 E1", the player's title is the episode's name with "The Simpsons · S4 E1" beneath it, and Downloads lists episodes by name with TMDB's screenshot (a season's picture is its first episode's screenshot). All go through `MetadataRepository.cachedEpisode` and `episode`: the device's answer first, otherwise one show search and one season request, both kept, so every other episode of that season is known after. Nothing waits for it: the number and the provider's picture show until the name arrives, and nothing is asked offline. On a show's page, an episode TMDB has no name for uses the words after the number in the provider's title ("Severance S01E10 - The We We Are" reads "The We We Are", through `Episodes.title`), and a title that is only the show and its number reads "Episode 1" once rather than "Episode 1" over "Harbour Town S01E01" (2026-11-18, UI review).

## Backup copies (2026-11-17)

From the app audit, Melvin picked "backup streams per channel". Providers list one channel several times ("ESPN", "US| ESPN HD", "US| ESPN FHD", "ESPN (Backup)"), and when one copy dies another usually works.

- **Which channels are copies** (`core/ChannelCopies`, with tests). The name minus a known country tag in front, picture and codec words (HD, FHD, 4K, HEVC, 1080p, 60fps), backup markers ("(B)", "[Backup]") and decorations ("ᴴᴰ"). Everything else stays, so "ESPN 2" and "ESPN News" are never copies of "ESPN", and "ABC - News" isn't a copy of "CBS - News" (ABC isn't a country). Copies must be from the same country, or one has no country tag. They are found through the full-text index (`ItemDao.copyCandidates`), then matched exactly, so no new column or migration was needed. Hidden channels are never used as copies.
- **When.** A live channel that dies for good: one that never showed a picture gets one retry (instead of five) so a dead channel is left quickly; one that was playing gets the usual retries. Then the copies are tried best first, ranked like auto-tune (recently dead last, then what played before, then the sharpest), each with the auto-tune wait for a picture. The first that plays takes the channel's spot in the list, so next and previous still flip through the same category. "Not working. Trying a backup copy (1 of 3)…" shows meanwhile, and "Switched to US| ESPN FHD" once one plays. Flipping to another channel stops the search.
- **One connection.** The old stream is stopped before a copy starts; nothing is fetched ahead.
- Settings › Player › **Switch to a backup copy**, on by default. Not used for auto-tune (which has its own list), recordings or while casting.

## TV playback (2026-11-17)

From the app audit, Melvin picked "match frame rate and Dolby audio fallback".

- **Match frame rate** (Settings › Player, on by default). When Media3 knows the video's frame rate, the player window asks for the display mode at the same resolution whose refresh rate equals it, or failing that twice it (`core/FrameRates`, with tests: 50 fps on 50 Hz, 25 on 50, 23.976 on 23.976, 29.97 on 59.94). It goes through `WindowManager.LayoutParams.preferredDisplayModeId`, so the TV returns to its usual mode when the player closes. Nothing happens on screens without such modes, or while casting.
- **Silent channels** (`player/AudioCheck`). Many sports channels carry Dolby (AC-3, E-AC-3) or DTS sound that phones often can't decode. If the stream has another audio track the device can play, Tuned picks it. If it has none, a note names the format ("No sound: this device can't play this channel's Dolby Digital (AC-3) audio") once per channel, instead of leaving a silent picture. Decoder fallback is on, so a device whose first decoder fails tries its next one.
- **Sound delay** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "do 4", from the gap list). Settings › Player › Sound delay plays the sound up to 500 ms earlier or later than the picture, for a soundbar, a TV's own processing or a Bluetooth speaker that lags. The picture follows the sound's clock, so `player/AudioDelay` moves that clock rather than re-timing the sound. It applies to every channel, film and episode, and stays on the device (it isn't synced), because each device's speakers lag by their own amount. Off (in time) by default.
- **A software Dolby and DTS decoder** (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "do 4"). Media3's FFmpeg decoder, which Google doesn't publish, is built in CI from the androidx/media tag that matches the app's Media3 version, so the two never mix versions (`.github/actions/ffmpeg-decoder`, FFmpeg 6.0, LGPL 2.1, with only the Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus, TrueHD and DTS decoders). It's built once per Media3 version and kept in the Actions cache (about 15 minutes the first time, nothing after), and the Android build and Release both put it in the app. The player tries it after the device's own decoders (`EXTENSION_RENDERER_MODE_ON`), so a device that plays the sound itself, or passes it to a soundbar, still does; one that can't now plays it instead of falling back to another track or the "No sound" note. It adds a few megabytes to the GitHub APK (less from Play, which sends one device type's copy). A local build without the file builds and plays as before, without those decoders, since Media3 finds the decoder by name.

## Pause and rewind live TV (2026-11-17)

Melvin: "Can you add pause and scrub live TV support?" Live channels arrive as one endless MPEG-TS stream, which can't be seeked, so Tuned keeps its own copy (`player/LiveBuffer`, `player/LiveTimeshift`, cutting rules in `core/TsSegmenter` with tests):

- **Kept as it plays.** The player's data source for live channels passes the stream through untouched and writes a copy to the cache folder, cut at key frames into pieces of about 4 seconds (by the stream's clock, PCR), each starting with the stream's PAT and PMT, plus a live HLS playlist. Starting a channel is no slower: nothing waits for the copy. Pieces older than Settings › Player › Pause and rewind live TV (30 minutes by default; 15, 60 or Off) are deleted, and never more than 1 GB is kept. Changing channel starts afresh; closing the player deletes it all.
- **Pausing** switches the channel's spot in the list to the playlist on the device, at the picture on screen (the player's buffer is ahead of it), paused. From then on the usual controls work: play, rewind and fast-forward buttons, the seek bar, and fast-forwarding to the end catches up to live. Playback speed is held at 1×, so Media3 doesn't creep back to the live edge by itself. Pausing longer than what's kept lands at the oldest piece still there.
- **Jump to live** (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "since we can scrub through live tv and go back in time, there should be a jump to live button"). While a channel plays from the copy on the device, a "Live" button with a red dot shows with the controls at the bottom right (`player/JumpToLive`). A tap puts the channel's live link back and plays what is airing now, rather than the end of the copy, which runs a few seconds behind. The recorder stops before the player reconnects, so there is still one connection, and what was kept stays: pausing again can rewind into it. It doesn't show at live, in picture-in-picture or while casting.
- **One connection.** When it plays from the device, a recorder carries on receiving the channel, reconnecting if it drops (a new connection marks a discontinuity in the playlist). The player's stream and the recorder share one permit: the recorder opens only after the player's stream has closed, and switching channel stops the recorder before the next channel opens. Moving to another channel puts the live link back in its spot.
- **HLS channels too** (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "do 7"). A live `.m3u8` channel arrives as short pieces the player fetches one at a time. The player's data source reads each of the channel's playlists as it passes (`core/HlsPlaylist`), learns its pieces, and feeds each piece, once, into the same copy (`player/LiveBuffer`), so pause, rewind, Jump to live and Record work as on any channel. While it plays from the device the recorder fetches the media playlist the player was on and each new piece, one request at a time, so there is still one connection; a playlist link that stops working (an expired token) is found again from the channel's own link once. A piece from another quality marks a jump, as a reconnect does. Channels whose pieces can't be joined into one stream play as before, without the copy: encrypted pieces (`#EXT-X-KEY`), fMP4 (`#EXT-X-MAP`, `.m4s`), byte ranges, or the sound as its own rendition. Subtitle renditions are ignored.
- Not for catch-up recordings, which can seek already, or while casting.

## Recording (2026-11-17)

From the app audit, Melvin picked recording, with the rule that it records only the channel being watched, on the one connection.

- **Record** sits with the player tools on live TV channels. It saves the stream the player is already receiving (`player/LiveRecorder`, fed by `LiveBuffer`), so recording never opens a connection of its own and can't record a different channel. The file starts at the first whole TS packet (`core/TsAlign`, with tests), so it opens cleanly anywhere. Pausing live TV keeps recording, since the same stream carries on arriving.
- A small "REC" mark stays at the top left while it runs, even with the controls hidden, and leaves picture-in-picture alone.
- It stops on the button, a channel change (including a switch to a backup copy), closing the player, or when the device has less than 500 MB free. Each stop says "Recording saved to Downloads".
- Recordings are `.ts` files with a small `.json` beside them in app-specific storage (`data/Recordings`), not a table: they belong to the device and outlive a playlist refresh or a playlist switch. Signing out counts them with the downloads: they go when the person picks Delete and stay when they pick Keep ([onboarding](onboarding.md#sign-out)). One the app never finished (Android closed it, or a crash) is kept at the length it reached.
- They are listed under **Recordings** on the Downloads page, newest first, with the show from the guide, channel, start, length and size. A tap plays the file like a film (seek bar, no resume, no next and previous), offline too, and never waits on the "every connection in use" hold. The bin deletes it.
- Pause and rewind no longer has to be on: with it off the stream still passes through `LiveBuffer`, keeping nothing, so Record works either way.
- **Scheduled recordings** (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "how to do 6?", then "do 4, 6, 7, 8"). A Record button sits beside the reminder bell on every programme in a channel's guide that hasn't ended. The rule that keeps one connection: a scheduled recording uses the connection only while nothing plays, and watching always wins.
  - Each one is a WorkManager job (`work/ScheduledRecordings`, `RecordWorker`), like a reminder, so the schedule needs no table.
  - It runs from a little before the programme to a little after it. **Settings › Player › Start recordings early and end late**: 0, 1, 2 (the default), 5 or 10 minutes each side. A programme with no end records for 2 hours, and nothing records for more than 6 (`core/RecordingWindow`).
  - Two recordings can't overlap. A second one for the same time is refused with "Another recording is set for then", rather than cutting the first short.
  - If something is playing when it's due, it waits and records what's left once watching ends. When the player opens or a TV starts playing during a recording, it hangs up at once and keeps what it saved, and the player waits for that hang-up as it does for a download's.
  - While it records, downloads and channel scans wait for it, and a running download hangs up first. A notification ("Recording …") shows while it runs.
  - It receives the channel itself (`data/LiveCapture`). A TS stream starts at its first whole packet and reconnects if it drops. An HLS channel starts at the live edge in its best quality, one request at a time. Encrypted or fMP4 HLS can't be saved as one file, so it isn't recorded. Portal channels are asked for their stream first, as a box would.
  - The file goes to the same Recordings list as one made in the player, titled with the programme. It stops with less than 500 MB free.
  - It's Premium, like Record (a tap without Premium opens the Premium screen). Switching playlists and signing out cancel the schedule, since the channels belong to the playlist.
  - Android can start a background job a few minutes late when the phone is idle, which the early start covers.
- Not recorded: catch-up, casting, and HLS channels that can't be kept (encrypted, fMP4, separate sound; see above); the button doesn't show for them.
