# Admin

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

Melvin, 2026-11-19: "similar to liftsmith and gradlings, i think we need reports, to do, overview, traffic, acquisition, funnel, devices, revenue, codes, users, installs, flags, automations, system, log". The admin is Liftsmith's admin panel (`mjparinas/liftsmith`, `docs/decisions/analytics-and-the-admin-panel.md`) cut to what Tuned has, in the same order Melvin listed the sections.

## Where it lives

- **`/admin` on the server's website** (`devices/`, Vercel project `tuned`), never in the app. One page with no framework and no build step, like the rest of `devices/public`: `public/admin/index.html`, `admin.js`, `admin.css`, and the app icon as its favicon. It wears the app's colours (violet `#6D4AFF`, teal, Tuned ink; [branding](branding.md)), dark first like the app, light when the computer is set to light.
- **Server code** is in `devices/lib/admin/`, apart from the accounts code it reads (`lib/db.mjs`, `lib/accounts.mjs`, `lib/account-store.mjs`; [accounts](accounts.md)), so the two can change side by side. The arithmetic and every rule are pure modules with tests (`analytics`, `flags`, `codes`, `metrics`, `todo`, `system`, `totp`, `session`, `automations`, `api`, `public`); `data.mjs` is only SQL. Its tables are in `devices/schema-admin.sql`, run by hand after `schema.sql`, like it.
- **One function for the panel**, `api/admin/[action].mjs`, routes every `/api/admin/<action>`, so the admin adds one serverless function rather than fifteen.
- **Preview with made-up data**: `node devices/admin-preview.mjs` serves the page at `http://localhost:4173/admin` against `lib/admin/sample.mjs`, signed in as an admin (`PREVIEW_ROLE=support` for read-only). It is how the screenshots are taken. Not deployed: Vercel serves only `public/` and `api/`.

## Signing in

- **Google, through the website's own sign-in** (`/api/admin/signin` starts it with `{ to: "admin" }`, and `/api/auth/callback` comes back to `/admin`), then the email is checked: `ADMIN_EMAILS` gets full access, `SUPPORT_EMAILS` read-only. Anyone else is told their address isn't on the list. The accounts thread asked for the admin's session to stay separate from the website's, so it has its own cookie (`tuned_admin`, HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, kept only hashed in Redis), lasting 30 days from its last use and at most 90 from sign-in.
- **Two-step sign-in is required** (as Liftsmith's, because the panel shows people's emails and can give Premium away): a 6-digit code from an authenticator app (RFC 6238, one step of drift either way, a step never accepted twice) or one of 8 single-use recovery codes, kept only as hashes. Five wrong codes lock it for 15 minutes. `ADMIN_2FA=optional` lets an address without it in; one that has it on always needs it.
- **Every change needs an admin, comes from the admin's own page** (the `Origin` header), and is written to the Log with who did it. Support staff see the same panel with the buttons left out.

## The sections

- **Reports:** problem reports sent from the app's Settings › Report a problem (`POST /api/report`): the person's words, the crash or freeze report, the log the app already scrubbed of logins, the device. To read, Done, Dismissed and Everything, with a note.
- **To do:** worked out from the data each time (reports to read, refunds in 14 days, the hourly job not running, required settings missing on Vercel), each snoozed until tomorrow or next week, or dismissed; its key changes with the data, so a new report brings it back. Plus the admins' own tasks with a due date. Overview shows the first five as **Needs you**.
- **Overview:** new installs and accounts against the period before, active installs today, this week and this month, Premium accounts and seats in use, sales before and after fees, the monthly share of yearly plans, installs a day, installs by build.
- **Traffic:** the website's own counter: visitors (once a day, no cookies), page views, pages, referrers, `utm_` sources and campaigns, countries, browsers, systems, devices, and buttons pressed (`data-track` on the account page's sign-in, buy, pair-a-TV and code buttons).
- **Acquisition:** where installs came from (Play's install referrer `utm_` tags, or the build), campaigns, mediums, countries, languages, installs a day.
- **Funnel:** three funnels whose steps can never grow (`funnel` in `metrics.mjs`): getting going (installed, added a playlist, played something), paying (installed, saw Premium, bought it) and accounts (installed, signed in, saved a backup), plus the website (visited, pressed a button); Both builds, Google Play or GitHub. The biggest drop, once at least 5 people reached the step before it, is called out.
- **Devices:** phones, tablets and TVs; Android versions; app versions; builds; makers; models.
- **Revenue:** sales at list price (the stores and Stripe don't tell us tax), after fees (Play 15%, Stripe 2.9% + 30¢), the monthly share of yearly plans, refunds, by product and by store, purchases a day, what's active now, and **running costs** that admins type in (a yearly fee counts as a twelfth), with profit a month. Prices are in `PRICES` in `metrics.mjs` and must follow [premium](premium.md) and [accounts](accounts.md).
- **Codes:** codes that give **Premium for life** or **1 to 10 extra seats**, for a number of uses and optionally a number of days (`TUNED-XXXX-XXXX` at random, or typed). Redeemed on the account page while signed in (**Have a code?**, `POST /api/codes/redeem`); a code becomes a `grant` entitlement, so it counts like a purchase but never as revenue. Seats follow the account's Premium, so lifetime and yearly never mix ([accounts](accounts.md)): lifetime seats for lifetime, yearly seats (a year long) for yearly, never past ten seats, and Premium can't be given to an account that has it. One use per account. Codes can be turned off and on.
- **Users:** accounts by part of an email or an id, with totals; an account's devices and seats, purchases and grants, backups (when, from which device, how big, never the contents) and codes used. Admins can free a device's seat, give Premium for life, and take back what was given.
- **Installs:** the latest installs with search (id, model, source, campaign), build, country and Load more; an install's page has what it did (each event's count) and its latest 50 events. The owner's devices and CI's emulator are marked **test** there and left out of every number.
- **Flags:** switches the app reads from `GET /api/flags` (on open, keeping the last answer), for everyone or a share of installs (a stable hash of the flag and install, so raising the share only adds installs). They are mostly off-switches for features that lean on someone else's service (TMDB, subtitle search, Trakt, TV sign-in, account backups, auto-tune, channel scans, downloads, cast), plus the minutes between usage uploads and the oldest version that may run without updating. A row exists only once a flag is changed; Default removes it.
- **Automations:** emails to `ADMIN_EMAILS` through Resend: a new purchase, a refund, a problem report, five failed second-step codes in a day, and a morning digest (yesterday's installs, accounts, purchases, reports and visitors) after 13:00 UTC, 8 AM in Toronto in winter. Each switchable, each kind at most every 6 hours, with a test button. Vercel runs `/api/cron/admin` every hour (`CRON_SECRET`), which also deletes app events and page views older than 25 months.
- **System:** Neon (size, how long it took to answer), Upstash, CI on staging and main and the latest release (GitHub's public API; `GITHUB_TOKEN` only raises the rate limit), this deployment, failed second-step codes today, and every environment variable the server reads, by name only, never its value, with what it powers and whether it's set (pairs count only when whole).
- **Log:** every sign-in, second step and change, newest first, 100 at a time, by who and by kind.
- **Every section has its own address** (`/admin?tab=users&open=<id>`, `&days=` for the period), and every move goes in the browser's history, as in Liftsmith. The period picker (24 hours, 7, 30 and 90 days, a year) shows only where it changes something. From 1024 px the sections sit in a side rail in four groups (Needs you, Numbers, Money, People, Running it); below it they wrap as buttons, never scrolling sideways, and tables turn into one card per row under 768 px. Loading shows the shape of what's coming, never a bare "Loading".

## What the app and website send

Melvin, 2026-11-19, asked whether the app should send anonymous usage stats to fill these numbers: **"Yes, like Liftsmith"**: sent from an allow-list, on by default outside the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland and off inside them until switched on, with a switch in Settings.

- **The allow-list is the only way anything reaches the server** (`EVENTS` and `INSTALL_FIELDS` in `lib/admin/analytics.mjs`; the server drops every other event and property, and a test fails if a property is named after a channel, title, address, login or search). An install is a random id the app makes, never Android's advertising id. It carries the build, version, Android version, maker, model, kind (phone, tablet or TV), language, time zone, and Play's install referrer tags; the country comes from Vercel's header, never the address. Events are opening the app, adding a playlist (its kind only), the first play and later plays (the section, cast or downloaded), plays that failed (why, in a word), screens, features used, Premium seen and bought, a full seat list, account sign-in, backups, crashes, updates and sign-out. **Never** a channel, film, show, playlist address, server, login, search, or anything typed.
- **The website's counter** (`public/beacon.js`, `POST /api/pv`) is cookieless: a visitor is a hash of the address, the browser and the day with a secret salt (`ANALYTICS_SECRET`), kept only as that hash, so the same person is counted once a day and can't be followed from one day to the next. It skips bots and honours Global Privacy Control, and the admin's own pages never count.
- **Each endpoint is limited per address** (the address only hashed, for the hour) and answers 503 on any failure of ours, which the app takes as "try later".
- **Raw events and page views are kept 25 months**, then the hourly job deletes them.
- The app's side (the usage stats switch and upload, reading flags, sending a report from Report a problem) is listed in [scope](scope.md) until it merges.
