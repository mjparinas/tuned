# Onboarding and playlists

**Several playlists, added one at a time** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "support multiple playlist streams… adding the username and password login on the main page doesn't really make sense… make it easy for users to add those"). The first screen no longer asks for a server, username, password and two links at once.

- **Welcome** (first run only): the brand, one line ("Add a playlist from your TV provider to start watching"), and four cards for what providers hand out: **Xtream Codes login**, **Playlist link** (M3U or M3U8, with an optional guide link), **Playlist file** (an `.m3u` on the device) and **MAC portal** (below). Under them, **Restore from your Tuned account** brings back the playlists, profiles and every setting from another device in one step (Melvin, 2026-11-19; it was Restore from Google Drive until Drive was replaced by the account the same day, [accounts](accounts.md)). There is nothing else to get through: no tour, no permissions.
- **One form per kind**, each with an optional name ("Sports", "Living room"). The Xtream form starts empty in every build, with `http://server:port` as a hint (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "the xtream codes server should not default to eroks4k"; it used to fill in a default server in GitHub builds), and checks the login with the server's small account answer before opening the app.
- **The password can be shown** with the eye in the field. On a TV it shows by default, with the keyboard's visible-password mode, and a "Show password" checkbox row under the field that the remote can reach, since left and right inside the field only move the cursor (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "on the google tv xtream codes login, dont hide the password… we dont know if we mistyped it when we encounter an error").
- **Pasted links are read for what they are** (`core/PlaylistLink`). A `get.php` or `player_api.php` link with a username and password becomes an Xtream login, so catch-up and account status work too; pasted into the Xtream server box, it fills in all three fields. Anything else that looks like a web address is a plain link, with `http://` added when missing.
- **Files are copied into the app** (`files/playlists/`, one file each), so the playlist still loads after the original is moved or deleted, and refreshes read it from there instead of the network. A file that doesn't start like an M3U playlist is refused. Files aren't synced to other devices, since they belong to this one.
- **The guide of a plain playlist** is the link given, else the one its `#EXTM3U` line names (`x-tvg-url`, `url-tvg` or `tvg-url`), read while the playlist is saved. Without either, there is no guide, rather than asking the default Xtream server for one.

**Fits a TV** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "The tuned app on a real TV has the logo touching the top of the screen, push that down"; 2026-11-20: "Right side at the bottom is cut off"). On a screen too short for one column (a TV, or a phone held sideways), the welcome (a smaller logo, the title and the line), Restore from your Tuned account and the privacy line sit on the left, and the kinds sit on the right in the wider column as compact cards (less padding, a smaller summary line), so all five on a TV, Sign in with your phone included, show without scrolling. Larger text (a TV's font size setting) makes the right column scroll, and moving down with the remote brings each card wholly into view. A test draws it at the size of Melvin's TV (853 by 480 dp) with the phone card and checks every card is wholly inside the overscan margin, measured whole rather than where its column clips it, and that at 1.3 times the text size the last card still scrolls wholly into view.

**MAC portals (Stalker, Ministra)** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "do 5"): a fourth card, **MAC portal**, signs in the way a MAG set-top box does, with the portal's address and the MAC address the provider registered (typed any way: with colons, dashes or none). Signing in shakes hands with the portal and sends the box's profile; a MAC it doesn't know gets no token and is refused on the form. `core/Stalker` holds the parts that need no network (the portal's API addresses, tried most likely first, the MAC, the stored links) and `data/StalkerPortal` the calls, with a MAG box's user agent and cookie.

- **Live TV and the guide only, for now.** The portal lists every channel in one answer, read one value at a time so thousands of channels never sit in memory, and sorted into countries and adult categories like any playlist. The guide comes from the portal (`get_epg_info`, up to a week) unless a guide link is given on the form; extra guide links fill gaps as usual. Portals list films and shows a page of about fourteen at a time, which for a big library is thousands of calls before anything shows, so they are left out until there is a way to load them without slowing the first refresh.
- **A channel's stream is asked for as it opens.** Portals hand out a fresh link with a short-lived token each time, so a channel is stored as `stalker://itv/<id>?cmd=<the portal's command>` and turned into the real link just before it plays (`data/StalkerLinks`): in the player's data source on the loader thread (before the live rewind copy, so pause and rewind work), by channel scans and before casting to a TV. A token the portal stops taking is fetched again once. One connection still holds: the portal's API calls aren't streams.
- **The MAC is kept like a password**: encrypted with the login, masked in the log, and synced to the other devices with the other playlists.

**Settings › Playlists** is a full screen: every saved playlist with its name, kind and where it comes from (never the password), a check on the one in use, and Rename, Edit and Remove as buttons under it (a three-dot menu until 2026-11-19, Melvin: "avoid 3 dot drop downs"). Tapping another switches to it. "Add a playlist" opens the same four cards. Adding a playlist makes it the one in use. Editing the one in use reloads it; editing another only saves it. Removing the one in use switches to the next; removing the last one signs out (with a warning) and shows the welcome screen. The Settings row reads the playlist in use and how many are saved. The old Server and Username rows are gone, since they meant nothing for a link or a file; the server is edited from the playlist's Edit.

**One playlist at a time.** The app still holds one playlist's channels and guide (switching clears them and downloads the other), so lists, search and favourites never mix providers and the database stays small. When the first download fails, "Change playlist" opens Settings › Playlists instead of signing out.

**The backup in the Tuned account** carries every saved playlist except files ([accounts](accounts.md)). Backup files were removed (Melvin, 2026-11-18).

## Sign in on a TV with your phone

Melvin, 2026-11-19: "if i install this on an android tv, entering in the url to the iptv stream, username and password is very much a pain when you use the remote". From the plan he picked the TV code and QR, sending from the phone app, and the website form ("do a, b, d"), on the existing Vercel project ("use the vercel one"), sending everything from the app ("i want it to sync everything if possible"), free, and no playlist files ("leave out").

- **On the TV** the welcome screen (and Add a playlist) starts with a "Sign in with your phone" card, the first thing focused, which opens a QR code and an eight-character code (`K7QF-29MD`, `ui/onboarding/TvSignInScreen`) that lasts 10 minutes and then makes a new one by itself. The ways to type a playlist are still under the card, and "Type it on the TV instead" goes back to them. Phones don't show the card.
- **A phone without Tuned** scans the QR with its camera and gets `www.tunedplayer.com/tv`: the Xtream login or a playlist link, with a pasted `get.php` link filling in the login as in the app. Someone who can't scan types the code there instead, and both screens show the same four digits to check.
- **A phone with Tuned** opens the app from the QR (an app link) or from Settings › Playlists › Send to a TV (`ui/onboarding/SendToTvScreen`, where a typed code is checked with the four digits), and sends everything sync between devices sends ([sync](sync.md)): every saved playlist except files, settings, the profiles with their favourites, lists and watch later, channel edits, guide fixes, and the Trakt and OpenSubtitles sign-ins. It is free, unlike Drive sync, because it is a one-time copy at set-up.
- **The TV shows what arrived** (the playlist names and what else came, never a password) and saves it only on "Use it", so someone who reads the code off the screen can't add a playlist unseen; "Not mine" throws it away and shows a new code (`data/PhoneSignIn`). A TV with nothing on it takes everything; a TV already in use adds only the playlists and keeps its own settings and favourites. The playlist in use on the phone becomes the one in use on the TV. Without Premium the TV keeps only that one, since more than one playlist is Premium ([premium](premium.md)).

**Nobody but the TV can read it** (`core/TvHandoff`, `devices/public/tv/seal.js`, `devices/lib/tv.mjs`, all tested, and a test opens a box the page sealed with the Kotlin code). The TV makes a one-time P-256 key pair; the QR link carries the public key after `#`, which browsers never send to a server. The phone seals with ECDH, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM, with the code bound in. The relay keeps the key, a hash of the TV's poll secret and the sealed box under a hash of the code, for 10 minutes at most, deletes the box when the TV takes it, and limits codes and lookups per visitor. On the typed-code path the relay hands the phone the TV's key, so the four digits (from a hash of the key) prove it's the TV's own. P-256 rather than X25519 because every Android version Tuned runs on, Samsung's TV browsers since 2018 and every phone browser have it, so a Tizen Tuned can use the same relay. The page is served by the relay, so on the no-app path the page's code is what keeps the login private; it loads no third-party scripts and the project's Content-Security-Policy allows only its own.

**Not built, and why:** a phone keyboard linked to the TV (the Google TV and SmartThings apps already type into any field, and it still means typing the login), and a page served by the TV over Wi-Fi with no server (Samsung TV apps can't listen on a port, and it fails on guest Wi-Fi and phones on mobile data).

**App links** need the relay's `/.well-known/assetlinks.json` to list each signing key: it has the GitHub release key. The Play build's app-signing key goes there once the app is in Play Console; until then that build's QR opens the page, which links to the app with `tuned://tv`.

## Sign out

**Signing out resets Tuned to how it was installed** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "sign out should forget every playlist, downloaded channels, disconnects trakt, google drive, anything else that syncs, all settings", and "it should also remove who is using the account users, basically it should just reset the app to a preinstalled state"). Settings › Playlists › Sign out (`data/AppReset`) removes:

- every saved playlist and login, the OpenSubtitles account, and copied playlist files;
- the cached channels, guide and catch-up, and every table in the database (favourites, history, watch later, your own lists, channel edits, guide fixes, channel health), read from the database itself so a table added later is emptied too;
- every "Who's watching" profile, its avatar and PIN, and the parental PIN; one empty profile is left in use, as on a fresh install;
- every setting, the learned intros, the channel scan report, the TV home screen rows, subtitles, the picture cache and the app's cache;
- Trakt and the Tuned account: forgotten on this device. Trakt isn't asked to revoke its token, because the backup shares the Trakt sign-in across the person's devices. All background work stops first, so the backup can never be replaced by the emptied app.

**What stays:** Premium, which belongs to the Play account and the device seat rather than a playlist (Play is asked again straight after); the crash reports and log in Settings › Updates and advanced, for support; and **the backup in the Tuned account**. Sign-out only signs this device out of it, so signing back in on this or another device can bring everything back. Deleting it is a separate, explicit choice on the account page.

**Downloads are asked about** (Melvin, 2026-11-19): when any are saved, a second step asks "Also delete 12 downloads (8.4 GB)?", counting downloads and recordings and the space they take on the device, with **Delete** (the default, the filled button) and **Keep**. Backing out cancels the sign-out. Keep leaves finished downloads and recordings, with the TMDB details and artwork saved for them, so they still play offline; their ids come from the stream, so they join their films and episodes again when the same provider signs back in. Unfinished ones go, since finishing them needs the login, and every saved stream address (which can hold the login) is forgotten.

**It can't stop half way.** The reset writes a marker before it removes anything and clears it only once every step succeeded; every step is safe to run twice. If the app is closed or crashes part way, the login is forgotten as the app next starts, before the first screen is chosen, and the rest finishes in the background. The database is emptied in one transaction.

Removing the last playlist in Settings › Playlists still only forgets the playlist and its channels; Sign out is the full reset.
