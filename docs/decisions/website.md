# Website

The public website at **www.tunedplayer.com** (Melvin registered it 2026-11-19; until DNS points there it is also `tuned-devices.vercel.app`), served by the Vercel project `tuned-devices` from `devices/public/`. The account, TV sign-in, device list and admin pages live on the same site ([accounts](accounts.md), [premium](premium.md), [admin](admin.md)).

**The old GitHub Pages site is gone** (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "i dont think we need this build step anymore", "we have a full website now"). `mjparinas.github.io/tuned` was `docs/index.md` and `docs/privacy.md` published from `main`; `docs/index.md` is deleted and Pages is turned off in the repository's settings, so pushes to `main` no longer build it. `docs/privacy.md` stays as the source of `/privacy`. Every link, including the Google sign-in consent screen's privacy and home page, points at www.tunedplayer.com.

## One header and footer on every page (Melvin, 2026-11-19)

"Every page should have a header and footer, similar to the one on the home page." The header (the channel stack and name, Features, Blog, Account and Download) and the footer (links, the note that Tuned includes no channels, and the store trademarks) are written once, `siteHeader` and `siteFooter` in `lib/site-build.mjs`. The generated pages (landing page and blog) have them inline. The hand-written pages (`/account`, `/account/channels`, `/tv`, `/devices`, `/check`, `/watch`, `/app/signed-in`) load `site.css` and `web.css` and include `/chrome.js` twice, `<script src="/chrome.js" data-part="header"></script>` first in `<body>` and `data-part="footer"` last, which writes the same header and footer in place before the page draws. `chrome.js` is generated from the same functions, so a change to the header reaches every page. A new page does the same; a test checks the hand-written pages have both. The admin keeps its own look.

The account pages (`/account` and `/account/playlists`, `<main class="wide account">`, styled by `public/account/account.css`) are laid out like an app's settings on a computer:

- **A side menu** (`public/account/nav.js`, one list for both pages so they never disagree): Overview, Devices, Premium, Playlists and settings, Channels and guide (always on), Watch and Check my provider (only while the `web_player` flag is on; [web player](web.md)), Pair a TV, Backup, What Tuned does, and Sign out or delete. On the playlists page its entry is the page, with the page's own parts under it (Playlists and each settings group). A link to the other page lands on its section once that page has drawn it. On a phone the same menu is a row of chips under the title that scrolls sideways; the page itself never does.
- **Overview first**: who is signed in, then four tiles, Plan, Devices (seats in use), Playlists (how many) and Backup (when it was saved), each a link to its card, so the account reads at a glance.
- **Cards fill the rest of the header's 1120 px**, two to a row in page order; a card marked `span` takes the whole row (Overview, Devices, What Tuned does, Sign out or delete). Devices are tiles side by side, with their kind drawn as an icon and a bar under the seats line. Pair a TV and Have a code are a box with its button beside it, sent with Enter. - **Playlists and settings**: Add a playlist sits by the Playlists title; on a desktop the form opens beside the list instead of under it, and the settings groups fill two columns top to bottom, so a short group never leaves a hole beside a long one.

## Dropdowns are Tuned's own (Melvin, 2026-11-21)

Melvin, 2026-11-21, of the More seats card on `/account`: "something about this dropdown looks ugly, can you make it our own custom component. also spacing in that card looks a bit off to me", then "can you make changes to all the drop downs in the admin please". So no page shows the browser's own dropdown:

- `public/select.js` dresses each `<select>` as Tuned's dropdown: a field like the text fields with a chevron that turns, and a list on a card under it (above it when there's no room below), the highlighted option shaded and the chosen one in the accent with a tick. Its look is `public/select.css`, whose `--ts-` properties default to `site.css`'s colours; the admin and the provider check set their own.
- It keeps the `<select>`, hidden, as the source of truth, so a page's code still reads `.value`, sets options and listens for `change`, and forms still submit it. A page calls `enhanceSelect` on each one; the admin, which draws its fields as it goes, calls `watchSelects` once.
- It is the WAI-ARIA select-only combobox: the label focuses it; ↓, ↑, Enter, Space open it; arrows, Home, End and Page Up/Down move; Enter, Space and Tab choose; Escape closes without choosing; typing jumps to a match. `a11y-check.mjs` checks those keys on the More seats card as well as running axe on every page with a dropdown.

## The blog (Melvin, 2026-11-19)

"Can you add blog posts that talk about whats new for each release and the date", and "ensure that each blog post has a CTA action, link to the android app".

- **Posts are Markdown** with frontmatter (`title`, `description`, `date` as YYYY-MM-DD, `kind`) in `devices/site/posts/`. The file's path is its address: `posts/whats-new/tuned-1-13-0.md` is `/blog/whats-new/tuned-1-13-0/`, and each folder gets its own index page. Kinds: `release` (What's new), `guide`, `compare`, `roundup`; the blog's front page groups posts by kind. The Markdown has headings, lists, tables, links, images, quotes, and lines starting with `<` pass through as HTML; images go in `devices/public/blog/img/`.
- **Every post ends with the call to action** to download Tuned for Android (and the coming-soon stores), from the shared template, so no post adds its own.
- **One post per release.** The releases up to 1.13.0 were written from their GitHub release notes, with a hand-written opening each. From now on `release.yml`, after publishing a GitHub release from `main`, runs `devices/release-post.mjs` on the release's notes (`feat` under New, `perf` under Faster, `fix` under Fixed; CI, tests, docs, refactors and admin-only work left out), rebuilds the site, commits the post to `main` (so it goes live with the release, Pacific date) and merges `main` back into `staging`. An existing post is never overwritten, so a post can be written by hand before the release.
- An RSS feed is at `/blog/feed.xml`.
- **Guides and roundups** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "add them all", to the list of guides offered for the blog). `posts/guides/` (kind `guide`) has getting started (Xtream, M3U link or file, MAC portal, signing in on a TV with a phone), how-tos (the TV guide, catch-up, recording and downloads, casting, subtitles, profiles), device guides (Android TV, Google TV, Nvidia Shield, tablets), troubleshooting (buffering, an empty guide, the user agent, provider errors), Premium with seats and devices, privacy, and a glossary. `posts/roundups/` (kind `roundup`) has the best IPTV player for Android TV and TiviMate alternatives, which link to every comparison in `posts/vs/`. Every claim comes from this record and the app, and their screenshots are the app's own screens with sample data, in `devices/public/blog/img/guides/`. **A pull request that changes a feature, a setting's place or a price these posts describe updates the post too**, as it does this record.

## How it's built

No framework and no build step on Vercel, like the rest of `devices/`. The site and server run on Node 26 (Melvin, 2026-11-20, from 22, to match the Vercel project's 26.x setting): `engines` in `devices/package.json` and CI's Node jobs say 26, so Vercel, CI and the tests run the same version. `node devices/build-site.mjs` turns `devices/site/` (`config.mjs`, `features.mjs`, `home.html`, `posts/`) into `devices/public/index.html`, `public/blog/**`, `sitemap.xml`, `blog/feed.xml` and `features.json`, with the shared header, footer, canonical address, Open Graph tags and structured data (`lib/site-build.mjs`). The output is committed; the test `devices/lib/site-build.test.mjs` (CI's Node tests) fails when it is out of date, and Prettier skips the generated pages.

- **`sitemap.xml`** lists the landing page, the blog, each folder's index and every post (with `updated` as its date when a post has one), on `https://www.tunedplayer.com`. **`robots.txt`** keeps crawlers out of `/admin` and `/api/` only and names the sitemap. The account, TV sign-in, device and `/app/` pages may be crawled so search engines read their `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` header (`vercel.json`) and leave them out of results; blocking them in `robots.txt` would hide that header and let their addresses be listed anyway.

## Search (Melvin, 2026-11-20)

"Go through the website and improve seo." What every generated page carries, from `lib/site-build.mjs`, so a new post gets it without doing anything:

- **Its own title and description.** The tab title is the post's heading, with " · Tuned" added only when the heading doesn't already say Tuned. A post whose heading isn't what people search for sets `seoTitle` in its frontmatter (the guides add "IPTV" this way, as in "How to fix IPTV buffering in Tuned"), and every comparison is titled "Tuned vs X: features and price compared". The blog and each section have their own search title and description (`KINDS` in `site-build.mjs`). A release post's description is its first sentence and then its first changes, never just "9 fixed" (`releaseDescription`). The test fails a page without one h1, with a title another page has, or with a description under 50 or over 200 characters.
- **A canonical address, Open Graph and X cards** (title, description, `og:locale` en_CA, `img/og.png` at 1200 by 630 with its alt text) and, on posts, `article:published_time` and `modified_time`.
- **Structured data:** `SoftwareApplication` (free, Android 8.0 or later, its screenshots and feature list), `Organization`, `WebSite` and `FAQPage` on the landing page; `BlogPosting` and `BreadcrumbList` (Home › Blog › section › post) on posts; `Blog` or `CollectionPage` with breadcrumbs on the blog's pages. No ratings or reviews are marked up, because there are none to cite.
- **Links between posts.** "More from the blog" under each post picks posts on the same subject (`relatedPosts`): a guide, comparison or roundup links to the two closest posts of its kind, by shared words and then the next in order so every post gets linked, and the closest post of another kind; a release links to the releases before and after it and a guide. The footer links to the guides and the comparisons, so both are one click from every page.
- **Pictures** have alt text and a width and height (the test checks every `<img>`). A post can set `updated: YYYY-MM-DD` when it is rechecked; it shows under the date and goes into the sitemap and structured data.
- **`/404.html`** is a page with the header and footer, links back to the home page, guides and comparisons, and `noindex`.
- **No hreflang**: the site is in one language (`en-CA`). Add it only when a translated site exists.

## Accessibility and standards (Melvin, 2026-11-20)

"Check the website for wcag, accessability, and other web standards." Every page, the account and admin included, meets **WCAG 2.2 AA** in light and dark, and CI keeps it that way.

- **The check** is `node devices/a11y-check.mjs`, run in CI's Formatting job whenever the website changes (about a minute, Chromium cached). It serves `devices/public` as Vercel does and opens every page in light and dark with reduced motion: axe-core's WCAG 2.2 A and AA rules and its best practices, the account page signed out and signed in (a made-up account), and every admin section with `admin-preview.mjs`'s sample data. At 320 px wide no page may scroll sideways (1.4.10), and everything Tab reaches must show a focus ring (2.4.7). Any finding fails CI. A new page is checked without being listed; a page that draws more when a button is pressed (like the admin's sections) has its states added to the check.
- **What the 2026-11-20 audit fixed**: the admin's faint text, violet links and pills, and its green, teal and amber pills were below 4.5:1 and are darker in light and lighter in dark; white on the admin's violet in dark is now 4.5:1. Form fields on the site have an edge of 3:1 against their card (1.4.11) and the same focus ring as buttons, where focus used to show only as a faint border colour. Folder pages of the blog went from h1 straight to h3 (now h2), and comparison tables had an empty heading in their corner; their first column is now row headings, and a table that scrolls sideways can take focus so a keyboard can scroll it. The admin's button columns have a heading a screen reader hears ("Actions"), long names wrap on a phone instead of pushing the admin sideways, and its search box shows focus. Reduced motion turns off every transition on the site. The provider check's dark button text is dark on its light blue.
- **Standards already met, kept**: `lang="en-CA"` on every page, one h1, landmarks (header, nav, main, footer) and a skip link, titles, descriptions, canonical addresses, Open Graph and Twitter cards and structured data on the public pages, `noindex` on the account, TV, device and admin pages, and Prettier-formatted HTML. Pages in `/account`, `/tv` and `/devices` get their headings filled in by script, which validators flag on the raw file; that is expected.
- **Security headers** (`devices/vercel.json`): `Strict-Transport-Security` (two years) and a `Permissions-Policy` that turns off camera, microphone, location, payment and USB were added beside the existing content security policy, `nosniff`, no referrer and no framing.
