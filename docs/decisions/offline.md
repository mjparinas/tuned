# Offline downloads

**What** (backlog, 2026-11-17). On phones and tablets, films and episodes can be saved to the device and watched without a connection. A download button sits on the film page and beside each episode, and a show's season has "Download season", which queues every episode in order (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "can we add download a full season"). Android TV does not show download actions or the Downloads page (Melvin, 2026-11-22: "remove download functionality on android tv").

**The Downloads page** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "there should be a downloads page"). On phones and tablets, Downloads sits in the bar at the bottom of the home screen, or in the rail, beside Live TV, Movies and Shows, with a badge counting what's still to download. It opens as a section of Home in their place, so the bar stays below it with Downloads lit, a tab leaves it, and back returns to the tab before (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "for downloads screen, why does the bottom navigation go away?"); before, it opened as a page on top of Home that hid the bar. As a section its title bar has no back arrow. The offline banner's Downloads button on any other phone or tablet screen still opens it as a page of its own, with a back arrow. Android TV has neither entry point. Settings has no link to it (removed 2026-11-18, Melvin: "settings doesn't need downloads since it already has it on the home screen"). Its layout is under "Shows, then seasons, then episodes" below. A download on its way shows progress (bytes of the full size, and about how much longer once it has been moving long enough to guess; Melvin, 2026-11-22), pause or resume, and cancel; a saved film plays on a tap and has a bin under its poster (its long-press menu removes it too), and a saved episode has a bin. A paused download is skipped until it's resumed, and the others carry on.

**One connection at a time** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: the app never holds more than one stream connection). Downloads run one after another in a WorkManager job, and the job is cancelled the moment the player opens. It starts again when the player closes, and the film that was interrupted carries on from the bytes it already had (2026-11-18): the partial file and the server's validator (`.part.tag`) are kept, and an HTTP range request with `If-Range` resumes it, so a changed file starts over rather than being stitched together. Before, every play threw a half-downloaded film away. Nothing starts a download while the player is open, even one added from the app behind picture-in-picture: it waits for the player to close, and the stream is released before the queue carries on. **Watching comes first** (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "If somebody is watching a show and they are downloading shows, the app should prioritize the stream and have the downloads wait. Remember only 1 stream at a time"). A stopped download hangs its connection up at once (`Downloader` cancels the OkHttp call when its coroutine stops, instead of waiting for the server's next bytes), and the player waits up to 2 seconds for that before opening its stream (`AppContainer.downloadOpen`, set by `OfflineWorker`). Casting counts as watching: the TV's stream is the account's connection and it carries on while the app is in the background, so downloads and channel scans wait until the TV is let go of (`player/TvCasting`, from Android's media router, for Chromecast and DLNA), and then carry on. A download stopped this way is never marked failed. A dropped connection during a download is retried and resumed with an HTTP range request when the server allows it.

**Where files go.** App-specific external storage (`getExternalFilesDir("offline")`), so no storage permission is needed and Android removes the files with the app. They aren't in backups.

**Network.** Downloads follow the existing "Wi-Fi only" setting for refreshes rather than adding a second one: a person who keeps refreshes off mobile data wants the same for gigabyte-sized files.

**Playing.** When a film or episode has a finished download, the player plays the file instead of the stream, with no other change, from every way in: the episode row, continue watching, the next episode, and the Downloads page. The player reads through Media3's `DefaultDataSource`, which opens `file://` itself and passes only network addresses to OkHttp; OkHttp alone can't open a file, so saved episodes used to fail (Melvin, 2026-11-17). A saved item never needs the provider, so it plays even while another download holds the one connection. Live channels can't be downloaded.

**Database.** Rows live in the `downloads` table (Room v6), keyed by the item's stable id, so they survive playlist refreshes like favourites do. Switching to a different playlist clears its items, so its downloads stay listed but can't be opened until that playlist is used again.

**Offline mode** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "If the app detects that it's offline, it should show you an offline banner at the top"). `data/Connectivity.kt` follows Android's default network: online means it has internet and passed Android's own check (validated), the test the channel scan uses. Going offline is reported after 3 seconds, so moving from Wi-Fi to mobile data doesn't flash anything; coming back, or starting offline, is reported at once. While offline a strip sits across the top of every screen except the player ("You're offline. Saved films and episodes still play."), with a Downloads button except on sign-in and on Downloads itself. It takes the status bar's space, so the screen under it doesn't move twice. Everything cached keeps working as it does online: the lists, the guide, search, film and show pages, posters (see Artwork below). Tapping something that isn't saved shows a short note instead of opening a player that can only fail; a tap is blocked only when the banner and Android's answer at that moment agree, so a network switch never stops one. A saved film or episode plays as always; offline, next and previous keep to the saved episodes, and a saved file never waits on the "every connection is in use" hold, since it opens no connection.

**Artwork and details, cache first** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "whenever you show them, you should grab from the cache first … if it's not available, that's when you actually go to TMDB"; and cast photos "should also be cached as well"). Posters, covers, backdrops, logos and cast photos go through Coil's disk cache in app storage (5% of the storage, 512 MB to 2 GB, least recently used out first); Coil 3 reads it before the network and ignores servers' cache headers, so a picture downloads once. TMDB answers live in the `metadata` table, and `core/MetadataCache.kt` decides when to ask: what the device has always shows first, an answer over a month old (a week for "nothing found") is refreshed behind it instead of making the page wait, and nothing is asked offline. Before a film or episode downloads, its page is saved too (`data/SavedArtwork.kt`): the TMDB details and every picture on the page, so it looks the same offline. Nothing is fetched ahead for things never opened or saved, which would cost far more traffic than it saves.

**Shows, then seasons, then episodes** (Melvin, 2026-11-17: "if it's a TV show, it should just show that one show with the TV show poster", then its seasons, then its episodes "with screenshots"). The Downloads page shows each show once as a poster, in the same grid as the Movies and Shows tabs (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "show them in poster view"), with a line such as "5 episodes · 1.7 GB · 2 downloading" and a bar while episodes are still on their way (Melvin, 2026-11-18: "I still want to see things like download status of a season"). The poster is the show's main cover (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "Shows on the download page should use the shows main cover/poster"): TMDB's poster for the show, which is saved with its episodes before they download (see "Artwork and details" above), so it shows offline too; only without one does the show's picture from the playlist stand in (the one on the Shows tab, which keeps an opened Xtream show's own cover; see [Playlist](playlist.md)). Before, the page could show an episode's screenshot instead. A tap opens its seasons, each with the picture of its first episode; a tap on a season opens its episodes in order, each with its screenshot, playing on a tap or showing progress, pause and cancel. Back goes up one level. Films are posters under Movies, those on their way first: a saved one plays on a tap and is removed from its long-press menu; one on its way shows its progress, pause or resume, and cancel under the poster. Names drop the provider's language tag ("The Simpsons (1989)", not "EN - The Simpsons (1989)"; Melvin, 2026-11-18), as everywhere else titles are shown. An episode whose show has left the playlist is listed with the films. Shows still downloading come first, then the most recently saved.

**Space** (Melvin, 2026-11-17). The top of the page says how much the downloads take and how much space is left on the device, with a bar.

**Recordings** (2026-11-17) of live channels sit in their own part of the Downloads page, under the films; see [Player › Recording](player.md#recording-2026-11-17).
