# Android TV

**What** (backlog, 2026-11-17: "Android TV remote support"). The same APK installs on Android TV and Google TV, shows a banner on the TV home screen, and works with a remote alone.

**One app, not a second one.** The phone screens already work with a remote: Compose moves focus between buttons, cards and rows with the arrows, and centre or OK clicks. On phones, a long press opens the same menu as a long touch, and every action there is also a button on the channel's or film's page (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "Optimize TV for remotes, avoid 3 dot drop downs"). **On Android TV there are no long-press menus** (2026-11-22): OK plays a channel or opens a film or show page, where favourite, lists and Play already sit; few people find a long press on a remote. A separate Compose for TV interface (`androidx.tv`) would mean a second copy of every screen to keep in step, so instead:

- Everything a remote can land on (posters, channel rows, Continue Watching cards, search results, episodes, guide rows) shows a clear outline in the accent colour while focused (`ui/common/FocusRing.kt`). Touch never focuses these, so phones look the same.
- The manifest says touch, Wi-Fi and leanback are optional, adds the TV launcher category, and gives the app a 320 × 180 banner (`drawable/tv_banner.xml`, a vector, so it stays sharp at 4K).

**Overscan margin** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "The tuned app on a real TV has the logo touching the top of the screen, push that down"). Many TVs crop the edges of the picture, so on a TV every screen of the app and of Premium keeps a margin: by default 16 dp at the top and bottom and 12 dp at the sides (tighter than Google's 27 / 48, and than the earlier 27 / 24; Melvin, 2026-11-22: still too much padding around the edge), filled with the theme's background (`ui/common/TvSafeArea.kt`). A screen with its own backdrop (the glow behind "Who's watching?") draws it on a layer that bleeds into the margin with `bleedIntoTvMargin`, so the backdrop reaches the screen's edges and only the content keeps the margin (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "Why doesn't the background cover the whole screen, there is a thick white boarder"). It is decided by the system's TV UI mode, so phones and tablets are unchanged. The player stays full screen: the crop only trims the picture's edge.

**Calibrate screen** (Melvin, 2026-11-22: reduce the padding "without impacting parts that the tv might remove", and "add a calibrate screen functionality that writes lines in the corners and allows you to move the image left right up down or stretch or shrink the screen", saved and backed up). Settings › Appearance › Calibrate screen (TV only) draws L-marks in every corner of the safe area and three remote-friendly sliders: move left or right, move up or down, and stretch or shrink. Changes save as they go (`tv_offset_x`, `tv_offset_y`, `tv_zoom` in `AppSettings`, backed up with the Tuned account so a reinstall keeps them), and every screen — including Premium and a TV's full-screen sheet — reads them through `LocalTvCalibration` into `TvSafeArea`. Reset puts the defaults back. The player stays full screen and is not calibrated.

**Tuned's logo in the upper left** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "Since there's so much real estate on TV, I want to see the tuned logo in the upper left-hand corner of the screen"). On a TV, Tuned's mark (the app icon on its dark square, which reads on both themes) and its name in the theme's text colour sit in the upper left of every screen (`ui/common/TvBrand.kt`): on Home, at the head of the rail above Live TV, the name under the mark; on every other screen with a title bar (Settings and its pages, a show, a person, a channel's guide, Events, Premium), before the back arrow and the title. A screen shows it once: Downloads and the film, show and guide panes beside Home's list leave it out of their own bars because the rail already has it. It takes no focus, so the remote never lands on it. On a short TV (Melvin's Shield, 480 dp tall) the rail's items scroll under it rather than Settings being cut off. The player never shows it. Phones and tablets are unchanged.

**Dark by default** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "Is there a way to set the TV app to dark mode by default?"). On a TV, Settings › Appearance › Theme left at "System default" means dark, because TVs such as the Shield report a light system theme that glares in a dark room; the window before the first frame is dark too (`values-television`). Picking Light there still gives light, and phones and tablets still follow the system (`ui/theme/Theme.kt`, `TvBrandTest`).

**The first download fits without scrolling** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "At the very bottom, there's like descriptive text that is slightly cut off at the very bottom"). Seven numbered steps stacked under "Getting your channels ready…" ran taller than the room under Home's search row on Melvin's TV (about 853 by 480 dp, less the margin and the row), and nothing on that screen takes focus, so a remote couldn't scroll to the line under them. On a short, wide screen (at least 600 dp wide and under 560 dp tall: a TV, or a phone held sideways) the title and that line sit on the left and the steps on the right (`FirstRun` in `ui/browse/HomeScreen.kt`, `FirstDownload` in `ui/browse/SyncSteps.kt`). `FirstDownloadTvTest` draws it inside Home's rail and search row at 853 by 480 dp and checks every step and the line show whole inside the margin, whichever step is running. A screen with nothing to focus must fit a TV whole, since the remote can't scroll it.

**No menus on Home** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "tv needs to be more stream lined and things should be easily accessible. especially on the tv, you have plenty of real estate"). A TV always shows the side rail, and the three-dot menu beside search is gone: Search and Settings are on the rail under the browse tabs (Downloads stays off a TV), then, with more than one profile, the profile watching, last on the rail under its own name and picture rather than "Profile" (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "move profile all the way to the bottom of the icons list. And instead of it saying profile on Android TV, it should actually say my name"), and Live TV has a button beside search for the guide grid. See [Large screens](large-screens.md).

**Channels cannot be renamed or have their logo changed on Android TV** (Melvin, 2026-11-22: "Don't allow renaming channels on android tv", then "android tv dont change logo"). The channel guide page and the channel row's long-press menu leave out **Rename channel** and **Change logo**, and the edit picker refuses both on a TV. Favourites and Add to list stay. Phones, tablets and the website keep rename and logo change ([lists](lists.md), [accounts](accounts.md)).

**List rename stays off the remote** (Melvin, 2026-11-22: "rename only allowed in android tv settings", then watch-first admin). Browse chips never rename or delete a list; **Manage lists** in Settings › Channels and lists deletes on a TV and leaves rename to the phone or website ([settings](settings.md)). Favourites stay one tap on every platform.

**No downloads on Android TV** (Melvin, 2026-11-22: "remove download functionality on android tv"). Film, episode and season pages leave out every download action, and Home's rail and the offline banner leave out the Downloads page. Phones and tablets keep downloads ([offline](offline.md)).

**No bottom sheets on a TV** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "Dragging handles shouldn't really be on the TV app", of What's new, and of Live TV's Filters: "I can't click to any of these selections in the filters, and when I click down or anything, the guide like kind of gets choppy"). A bottom sheet opened half way with a drag handle a remote can't drag, and the remote's focus slipped out of it into the guide behind, which scrolled under it. Every panel that slides up on a phone is `TunedSheet` (`ui/common/TunedSheet.kt`; detekt forbids Material's `ModalBottomSheet` elsewhere): on a TV it is its own full-screen window inside the overscan margin, so focus stays inside, every option is reachable with the arrows, the list scrolls as focus moves, and Back closes it. Filters opens with the remote on the first type, each chip outlined while focused; What's new lays the header and Done on the left and the changes on the right ([updates](updates.md)). `TunedSheetTest` and `WhatsNewTvTest` check there is no drag handle and the first option has focus.

**Where focus goes** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "Optimize TV for remotes", from the UX audit; `ui/common/TvFocus.kt`, paging rule in `core/ListPaging` with tests). On a TV only:

- Live TV, Movies and Shows open with the remote on the item last focused there, or the first one, so the first press of an arrow moves from something visible rather than from nothing. Closing a film, show or guide pane beside the list (or coming back from its page) puts focus back on the item that opened it.
- A film's page opens with the remote on Play (or Resume), and a show's page on the season showing (or its first episode, when there is only one season), so OK does the likely thing at once.
- A show never opens in Home's right-hand detail pane on a TV (Melvin, 2026-11-22: "I don't like the right hand slide out for a tv show", then "make seasons take a full width" and "shows should show the hero information (cinematic view) similar to a movie"). It opens full screen: the same cinematic hero as a film (backdrop, poster, title, rating, trailer, watch later, summary and cast), then full-width season tabs the remote can move across, then a grid of the selected season's episodes as 16:9 screenshots with each episode's title. Changing a tab swaps the episode grid. TMDB's episode screenshot is preferred, with the provider's episode image underneath it while metadata is unavailable. Tablets keep the list-and-detail pane.
- "Who's watching?" opens with the remote on the first profile, so OK alone picks it (Melvin, 2026-11-20: "when you're choosing a profile and there are multiple, it should auto highlight the first entry so we only need to click ok on the remote"). It is the first in the row, not the one last used, so the same press always picks the same profile (`ProfilesTvFocusTest`). The highlight is the same 3 dp accent ring on the picture as on the phone, not a thick tile-wide frame (Melvin, 2026-11-22; [profiles](profiles.md)).
- Page up and down, and channel up and down, jump a screenful through a list or grid rather than one row, which matters with thousands of channels. Channel up goes up the list, as on a cable box.
- Back while scrolled down goes to the top of the list first, as other TV apps do; the next Back leaves as before. A pane or dialog beside the list still closes first.
- Focus is only put back within a moment of a page opening, so an item that scrolls into view later never pulls focus away from where the person is.

**Search boxes and the remote** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: on TV search "it asks me to use my keyboard. I don't know how to do that ... there is a speaker icon. When I click right ... I can't access that speaker icon"; then 2026-11-22: "i dont want to see this use the keyboard on your device functionality. if the user selects a text input, a virtual keyboard should show up" and "when the microphone is pressed, another input box and microphone show up as well. neither microphone allows the app to record voice"; `ui/common/RemoteTextInput.kt`, `ui/common/TvKeyboard.kt`, `ui/common/VoiceSearch.kt`, `RemoteTextInputTest`). On a TV the per-tab search boxes on Live TV, Movies and Shows stay off — Search is already on the rail — and Live TV keeps only the guide-grid toggle in that top bar. Every remaining search box (Search everything on the rail, Settings › Countries and languages) works with a remote alone:

- OK opens Tuned's own D-pad keyboard (`TvKeyboardHost` above navigation), from the D-pad's centre, Enter or a game pad's A (the Shield controller). The field is read-only on a TV, so Android's input method — and its "use the keyboard on your device" strip — never opens. Compose on its own only answered the centre key from a physical D-pad.
- The box's buttons sit beside the box on a TV rather than inside it, because the remote can only move to something outside the box. The mic is on the left of the box and Clear (or Add) on the right, as on other TV apps (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "in many apps the mic is on the left and the search box is on the right"), so Left from the box reaches the mic and Right reaches Clear. The mic stays while there is text, so focus is never lost when voice fills the box.
- Search everything opens with the remote on the mic, so OK alone starts talking (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "on android tv, the mic should be selected by default"); on a TV without voice search it opens on the box. The Live TV, Movies and Shows tabs keep the remote on the list (no tab search box).
- The mic records with Android's inline `SpeechRecognizer` in Tuned's own search row (asking for the microphone when needed), never the recogniser activity that draws a second search box and mic. The words fill the box. Phones and tablets still use the recogniser activity.
- The box and each button show the accent ring while the remote is on them. Left and Right move between them rather than a cursor no one can see; Tuned's keyboard has its own arrows.

**Live TV uses the big screen** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "use the big screen", from the UX audit). On a TV, the groups (Live events, Filters, All, Favourites, your lists, Recent and the provider's groups) always run horizontally across the top in both the channel list and guide grid (Melvin, 2026-11-22: "ensure it always shows up horizontally"). Beside the channel list are the details of the one under the remote's focus: its logo, number and name, what's on now with the time and how far through it is, and what's next, each with its description from the guide (`ui/browse/ChannelInfo.kt`). The details are words and pictures from the guide on the device, never a preview of the channel, so there is still one stream connection ([scope](scope.md)); they take no focus, so the arrows stay in the list. They are left out when a TV is too narrow for both a readable list and the details (a film or guide pane open beside the list, say). On a TV the guide grid's channel column is 260 dp wide, against 116 dp on a phone, so long channel names show on up to two lines before they're cut (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "the channel name is mostly cut off. Can you widen the channel name section in the grid"); beside the rail a TV's grid is narrower than a tablet's, so it used to get the phone's column.

**Movies and Shows use the big screen** (extended 2026-11-22). On a TV their groups run down a column on the left, the poster grid in the middle, and beside it the details of the title under the remote's focus (`ui/browse/PosterInfoPane.kt`): poster, title, year, rating, runtime, genres and overview from the metadata already on the device (never a fresh TMDB call as the remote flips through), and for a show its episode count. The pane takes no focus; OK still opens the film or show page. Phones and tablets keep the row of chips.

**The remote in the player** (`core/RemoteKeys.kt`, with tests). Media3's controls already handle play, pause, fast forward and rewind, and any arrow shows the controls. On top of that:

- On live TV, up and down change channel while the controls are hidden, as on a cable box. The channel up and down buttons (and page up and down) change channel at any time.
- In films and episodes, left and right skip back and forward by the player's skip amounts while the controls are hidden.
- The guide (or menu) button opens and closes the mini guide on live TV, and in films and episodes the menu button shows the controls (Fire TV's remote has no guide or info button, [Fire TV](#fire-tv)); the info button shows the controls, with the stream's pills (stream info was removed 2026-11-19). Fit is a button in the player's top row, reached with the arrows like the others; there is no screen lock on a TV.
- Once the controls show, the arrows move between buttons as usual. While the screen is locked, keys do nothing but reveal the unlock button through back.

Holding an arrow keeps skipping; holding a channel button changes channel once, so a long press can't flip through several channels and open several connections in a row.

**Channel numbers and flip back** (2026-11-17, from the app audit). Live channels are numbered 1 up in playlist order (`items.number`). Each playlist refresh sets the numbers, and an older cache gets them once in the background (`ChannelNumbers`). The number shows beside each channel in Live TV (Settings › Channel numbers, on by default). In the player, typing a number on the remote changes to that channel 1.5 seconds after the last digit (`core/RemoteKeys`, `player/ChannelDial`). The last-channel button, or the flip-back button in the player's toolbar on phones, goes back to the channel watched before. Both change the stream the player already has, so they never open a second connection. Numbers follow the playlist rather than the provider's `tvg-chno`, which few playlists set and which can repeat.

**Start on the last channel** (2026-11-17, from the app audit). Settings › Start on the last channel (off by default) makes a fresh launch from the home screen or the TV launcher open the player on the live channel watched last, as a cable box does. The lists load from the cache underneath, and back shows them. A search from the Assistant, a widget or a link opens what it asked for instead. When "Who's watching?" opens at start (more than one profile, asked at every start), the app waits for the pick instead (2026-11-18): the last channel belonged to whoever watched last, so a kids profile could start on a grown-up's channel past its PIN.

**Home screen** (from the app audit, 2026-11-17). On Android TV, films and episodes in progress appear in the system's Continue watching row (Watch Next), and favourite channels in a Tuned row of their own (`work/TvHome.kt`, `androidx.tvprovider`). They are brought up to date a few seconds after the app opens, after playing and after a favourite changes, and removed on sign-out or a playlist switch. A card opens `MainActivity` with `ACTION_PLAY`, which starts the player with the lists underneath. Nothing runs on phones.

**Tunnelled playback** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: "do 8", from the gap list). Settings › Player › Tunnelled playback, shown on Android TV only, hands the picture and sound to the TV's own video hardware, which keeps them in step itself and can play 4K and 50 or 60 fps sport more smoothly on TVs whose decoders support it. Media3 uses it only when the TV's decoders for both the picture and the sound of that stream support it, and plays as usual otherwise, so turning it on can't stop a channel playing. Off by default, as in other players: some TVs report support and then stutter or lose sync, so it's there to try when playback isn't smooth. It stays on the device rather than syncing, and Settings › Player › Sound delay doesn't apply while it's on, because the hardware keeps its own clock.

**Phone-only settings stay off a TV** (Melvin, 2026-11-22: TV stays streamlined for the remote). Settings › Playback leaves out landscape, picture-in-picture, the mini player, background play and gestures, and Settings › Guide and refresh leaves out pull to refresh: none of those apply with a D-pad. Media buttons, frame-rate match, seek lengths, tunnelled playback and Refresh now stay. Phones and tablets keep the full list ([settings](settings.md)).

**Watch-first admin** (Melvin, 2026-11-22: phone and web generally do everything; Android TV stays streamlined and avoids typing). On a TV, playlist rename and edit, list rename, creating a new list, adding or renaming profiles, extra guide URLs, a custom user agent, typing a Live events follow, Forgot PIN (account password), the OpenSubtitles account, and the Settings search box stay on the phone or [tunedplayer.com](https://www.tunedplayer.com); each screen says so where needed. Guide match search keeps Tuned's D-pad keyboard. The TV still switches and removes playlists, deletes lists, unlocks a numeric PIN, picks a user-agent preset, ticks existing lists, and removes a follow chip. The welcome screen's "Type it on the TV instead" escape hatch stays ([onboarding](onboarding.md), [accounts](accounts.md)).

**TV smoke and startup** (2026-11-22). Robolectric TV-qualifier tests cover remote-first surfaces. Gradle Managed Devices do not support android-tv images, so CI device smoke and baseline cold-start stay on Pixel 6; a real TV box is the end-to-end check ([diagnostics](diagnostics.md), [speed](speed.md)). The smoke harness still skips download and mini-player journeys when it detects leanback.

**Settings with six buttons** (Melvin, 2026-11-21: "as I'm scrolling through the caption settings, as soon as I get to the size of the text, if I press up and down, it adjusts the size of the text rather than going to the next field", and "remote controls, you basically have up, down, left, right, OK, and back"). The audit of every Settings screen with a remote found, and fixed:

- **Sliders** (caption size, background opacity, position): up and down go to the row above or below, as on every other row; left and right move one step and save at once (`SliderRow`, `sliderStep`, `SettingsTvRemoteTest`). Material's slider took all four arrows, so nothing below the text size could be reached.
- **Where the remote is**: every Settings row, choice in a dialog, colour and accent swatch, and the rows of the Add to list, guide match and logo pickers shows the accent outline while focused (`focusRing`; white on the swatches, where the accent marks the colour chosen). Everything else Material draws (buttons, chips, icon buttons) gets a stronger focus shade on a TV (`TvFocusShade`), a quarter of the content colour rather than a tenth.
- **Where it starts**: Settings opens on the page shown beside the list, not the search box; a page opened on its own, and Captions, open on their first row; a choice opens on the one in use, so OK alone keeps it.
- **Captions on a wide screen** put the preview beside the rows: above them, on a TV 480 dp tall, it left room for about two rows.
- **Order favourites** keeps the remote on the channel being moved: after a move, focus goes to the same arrow on its new row, or to the other arrow once it reaches the top or bottom, so pressing OK again keeps moving it. Before, focus stayed on the old row, now another channel, and dropped when that arrow turned off.
- **Logs**: a remote can't select text and a list of plain text gives it nothing to land on, so the log couldn't be scrolled past the crash reports. On a TV each line takes focus with an outline; Copy and Share still take the whole log.

Checked and already fine: switches and choices toggle or open with OK; dialogs and pickers close with Back; text fields leave with the arrows and open the TV's keyboard with OK; there are no drag-only or long-press-only controls in Settings. Bottom sheets, search boxes and the mic, Check for updates and the side rail are covered on their own ([updates](updates.md), and the notes above).

**Signing in with a phone** (Melvin, 2026-11-19: entering the server, username and password "is very much a pain when you use the remote"). On a TV the welcome screen leads with "Sign in with your phone": a QR code and a short code, and the phone (with or without Tuned) does the typing ([onboarding](onboarding.md)).

## Fire TV

**Decided** (Melvin, 2026-11-27: "can you start building the fire tv version of this", reversing 2026-11-18's "not going to do fire tv for now"). Fire TV runs Fire OS, which is Android without Google's apps, so Tuned for Fire TV is the same app as a third build, `amazon`, for the Amazon Appstore, not a second codebase. Every TV rule above applies to it unchanged: Fire OS reports the TV UI mode, so the overscan margin, the rail, focus, Tuned's keyboard and the remote's keys all work as on Android TV.

- **Which Fire TVs.** Fire OS 7 (Android 9) and later, which is every Fire TV sold since 2019 and the older ones that took the update. Fire OS 6 and earlier (Android 7.1 and older) are below the app's Android 8.0 floor and can't install it. Fire TVs on Vega OS (Amazon's Linux system on some 2025 sticks) don't run Android apps at all and would need an app of their own; nothing is built for them.
- **No Google Play services**, so nothing in the `amazon` build depends on them: no Play Billing (it isn't in the build), no Play in-app updates (`ui/AppUpdates` asks nothing; the Appstore updates the app), no self-updater (the Appstore doesn't allow `REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES`, which its manifest drops as `play`'s does). Chromecast needs Play services, so casting from a Fire TV lists DLNA TVs only, as on any device without them (`CastControl` already treats a missing `CastContext` as no Chromecasts). Google sign-in for the Tuned account was always the TV device-code flow, not Play services, so it works. Voice search hides its mic where the system has no speech recogniser.
- **Premium comes with the Tuned account** (`app/src/amazon/.../StoreFor.kt`, `Store.sells` false, `Premium.SOLD_IN_APP`). The Fire TV app sells nothing: no prices, no buy buttons, no link to a store or the website, as Amazon expects of an app that unlocks what was bought elsewhere. Signing in to the Tuned account is never locked there (the backup itself stays Premium, as on Play, where signing in is: Back up now shows the lock and the background backup skips until Premium is on), and an account with Premium (bought on any device or the website) unlocks it with a seat, as on Play ([accounts](accounts.md)). The Premium screen says Premium comes with the account, shows the sign-in, and Check again asks the account with a spinner ("Couldn't reach your Tuned account" or "doesn't have Premium yet" when it can't). Signed out, Premium is off; signed in and offline, it keeps what the TV last heard, so a paying person is never locked out by an outage. Melvin chose this over Amazon In-App Purchasing and over leaving Fire TV unlocked (2026-11-27: "Tuned account only").
- **The remote.** A Fire TV remote has no guide or info button; its menu button (three lines) opens the mini guide on live TV, as before, and now shows the controls in films and episodes (`core/RemoteKeys`).
- **Builds.** `BuildConfig.STORE` (`github`, `play` or `amazon`) names the build to the server (`build` on a device and in the usage stats, filterable in the admin) and in Report a problem. CI's Android build compiles and shrinks the `amazon` release with the others; Release builds a signed `amazon` APK as the `tuned-amazon-apk` artifact, which goes to the Amazon Appstore's Developer Console by hand. The GitHub APK also installs on a Fire TV by hand (Downloader), with everything unlocked and its own updater, as on any Android TV.
